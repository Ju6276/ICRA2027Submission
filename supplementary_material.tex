\section{Training and Architecture Details}

\assumed{The teacher is trained for $50{,}000$ steps with AdamW at a peak learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$. It predicts $H{=}50$ actions at $30$~Hz from the latest $100$~ms of the $100$~Hz wrench stream, and flow-matching inference uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ steps.}

After freezing the teacher, \assumed{we train the rank-$32$ null-mode adapter for $10{,}000$ steps at a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. The visual--language prefix is pooled into $M{=}16$ intent tokens. The residual policy comprises a causal force encoder and one pre-norm attention block with separate zero-initialized force and staleness output projections. It predicts $K{=}5$ corrections and is trained for $30{,}000$ steps at a learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$ under replayed asynchronous schedules. The final implementation record will specify the intent-token pooling rule and the embedding used to mask the force token for the staleness head.}

The action representation and rotational residual composition are defined in Sec.~III-A of the main paper. Let $\widetilde S^p$ be the first nine pose coordinates of the normalized robot state, obtained after converting orientation to the continuous 6D representation, and let $\sigma_S^p$ and $\sigma_A^p$ be the corresponding training-set state and action standard deviations. The implementation computes
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma(S_t,S_k)=
    \frac{\sigma_S^p+\varepsilon}{\sigma_A^p+\varepsilon}
    \odot(\widetilde S_t^p-\widetilde S_k^p),
    \qquad \varepsilon=10^{-6}.
    \label{eq:supp_gamma}
\end{equation}
Thus, the rotational block is a scale-corrected componentwise difference in continuous 6D rotation coordinates, rather than an exact relative rotation on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. The composed normalized action is de-normalized, applied relative to the reference-query state $S_k$, and projected to a valid rotation by Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization.

The residual policy observes only the reference sample aligned with its query time. The remaining $K-1$ aligned reference samples are used for per-step target construction and command composition, but are not provided as student inputs.

At deployment, force-conditioned and staleness corrections are clipped separately and element-wise before composition. The implementation accepts either nine-dimensional nonnegative bounds $b_F,b_S\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{9}$ or scalar values broadcast over the pose coordinates; bimanual clipping is applied independently per arm. \assumed{The final robot configuration must specify the values used for $b_F$ and $b_S$.}

\assumed{Residual-policy training examples are anchored at the $30$~Hz state/action timestamps while retaining every $100$~Hz wrench sample in $\mathcal F_t$. Residual packets may be queried more frequently at deployment; the realized publication rate is measured directly rather than inferred from the waypoint rate.}

\subsection{Null-Mode Optimization}

The learned null token and null-specific adapter are active only for null-mode queries. With the force-conditioned teacher parameters $\theta$ fixed, the null-mode parameters $\phi$ are optimized using the teacher's original flow-matching objective,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{null}}
    =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,\eta,\epsilon}
      \!\left[\ell_{\mathrm{FM}}\!\left(
      T_{\theta,\phi}^{\mathrm{null}},
      A_{t:t+H-1}^*;\eta,\epsilon\right)\right],
    \label{eq:supp_null_training}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is flow time and $\epsilon$ is the initial flow noise. Full-force and null-force predictions used to construct a residual target share the same $\epsilon$.

\subsection{Residual-Loss Weighting}

For a $K$-step residual chunk, the normalized temporal weights are
\begin{equation}
    w_j=\frac{\beta^{j-1}}{\sum_{m=1}^{K}\beta^{m-1}},
    \qquad 0<\beta\leq1.
    \label{eq:supp_temporal_weights}
\end{equation}
Thus, $\beta<1$ emphasizes near-term corrections, $\beta=1$ weights all steps equally, and $\sum_jw_j=1$ keeps the loss scale stable as $K$ changes. The loss is a mean-squared error in normalized pose-action coordinates.

\section{Asynchronous Packet Execution}

\subsection{Reference Packets}

A reference query $k$, issued at $t_k$ from state $S_k$, publishes the immutable packet $(A_{T,k,1:H}^{\mathrm{null}},Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}},S_k,t_k,\bar t_k)$ when its result becomes available at $\bar t_k$. Its samples carry intended execution times
\begin{equation}
    t_{k,n}^{\mathrm{ref}}=t_k+(n-1)/f_A,
    \qquad n=1,\ldots,H.
    \label{eq:supp_reference_timestamps}
\end{equation}
At time $t$, the active reference is the freshest completed, non-superseded query,
\begin{equation}
    k^*(t)=\arg\max_{k:\,\bar t_k\leq t}t_k.
    \label{eq:supp_active_reference}
\end{equation}
An older query that completes later therefore cannot replace a newer active packet. Samples whose intended times precede $\bar t_{k^*}$ are skipped for direct execution; the cached chunk itself is not truncated. For $t\geq\bar t_{k^*}$, the reference pose $A_{k^*}^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ is obtained by timestamp-based interpolation over the original timestamped chunk.

\subsection{Residual Packets}

A residual inference packet $q$, initiated at $t_q^F$, reads $k_q=k^*(t_q^F)$ together with $(\mathcal F_{t_q^F},S_{t_q^F})$. It completes at $\bar t_q^F$ with force-conditioned and staleness correction chunks. Their samples are timestamped as
\begin{equation}
    t^F_{q,j}=t_q^F+(j-1)/f_A,
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,K.
    \label{eq:supp_residual_timestamps}
\end{equation}
Here $f_A$ defines the waypoint spacing within a packet; residual query times $t_q^F$ are scheduled independently. Step $j$ is held over $[t^F_{q,j},t^F_{q,j}+1/f_A)$, and correction samples are not interpolated. At control time $t$, the executor selects the most recently initiated packet that has completed and remains valid, together with its active step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    q^*(t)&=\arg\max_{q:\,\bar t_q^F\leq t<t_q^F+K/f_A}t_q^F,\\
    j(t;q^*)&=1+\left\lfloor(t-t_{q^*}^F)f_A\right\rfloor.
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:supp_active_residual_step}
\end{equation}
Any prefix that expires during inference is therefore skipped, while the remaining valid steps execute sequentially until a newer packet completes. Let $j^*=j(t;q^*)$. Within the selected interval, Eq.~(1) of the main paper is evaluated using the residual packet's query time and active step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal P(A^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t))
    \equiv{}&\mathcal P(A^{\mathrm{cmd}}_{t_{q^*}^F,j^*})\\
    ={}&\mathcal P\!\left(A_{k_{q^*}}^{\mathrm{ref}}
        (t^F_{q^*,j^*})\right)
      +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}_{q^*,j^*}
      +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}_{q^*,j^*}.
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:supp_active_residual}
\end{equation}
The two corrections are clipped independently and element-wise in normalized delta-action coordinates before composition. The composed command remains referenced to $S_{k_{q^*}}$ and is converted to an absolute robot command as described above. A packet that completes after $t_q^F+K/f_A$ is rejected. If no valid residual packet remains, the executor invokes the platform's safe hold or abort policy. \assumed{The final implementation record will specify gripper sampling and behavior beyond the last valid reference timestamp.}

\subsection{Metric Aggregation}

\assumed{Peak force is computed from every trial. For a bimanual trial, it is the maximum force norm over time and over both arms. Completion time is measured only on successful trials, from the first policy command to satisfaction of the task-specific success criterion. Platform-level values first aggregate trials within each task and then macro-average the five single-arm or four bimanual tasks. These conventions must be verified against the final evaluation code.}

\subsection{Seed-Wise Task Results}

\assumed{The final supplement will report the successful-trial count for each training seed, method, and task. Each count is out of ten trials and aggregates to the main paper's success-rate table.}
