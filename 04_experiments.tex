Our evaluation addresses three questions: whether the proposed factorization improves task performance over force-free, force-conditioned, and slow--fast baselines; whether the learned residual improves physical interaction during contact; and how the paired target, temporal force representation, and null-force construction contribute to performance.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\subsubsection{Hardware platforms.}
The single-arm platform consists of a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda manipulator observed by two Intel RealSense D405 cameras. We use a separate bimanual platform for the coordinated tasks described below.

\subsubsection{Tasks and data collection.}
We evaluate button pressing, object flipping, USB insertion, and cabinet-door opening with the single-arm platform, and plug insertion, plug removal, whiteboard wiping, and drawer opening with the bimanual platform (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). We collect $200$ and $300$ teleoperated demonstrations per single-arm and bimanual task, respectively. Each trajectory records synchronized multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions at $30$~Hz, together with timestamped six-axis force/torque measurements at $100$~Hz.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_baselines}

We compare against four published policies. $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05} is a vision--language policy without force input. ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla} fuses instantaneous force through a force-aware mixture of experts, and TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavla} conditions its decoder on torque history. ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2025implicitrdp} is a visual--force diffusion policy with structural slow--fast processing.

We evaluate two training seeds with ten trials per seed. We report per-task success rate and the mean over each platform. For successful trials, we additionally report peak contact force and task completion time, averaged separately over the single-arm and bimanual tasks. Lower values indicate safer interaction and faster execution, respectively.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp_implementation}

All models are trained on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs using bfloat16 precision. We fully fine-tune the teacher for $50{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $32$, a peak learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, $1{,}000$ warmup steps, cosine decay, and gradient clipping at $1.0$. The teacher predicts $H{=}50$ actions at $30$~Hz and encodes the latest $100$~ms of the $100$~Hz force/torque stream. Flow-matching inference uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ steps.

We freeze the teacher and jointly optimize the null token and rank-$32$ null-specific adapter for $10{,}000$ steps with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. The visual--language prefix is pooled into $M{=}16$ tokens. The residual student uses one pre-norm attention block and predicts $K{=}5$ corrections. It is trained for $30{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $128$, and a learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$. We set $\beta=0.5$.

\subsubsection{Qualitative results.}

We present representative successful and failed rollouts from both platforms. Each sequence is aligned with the measured force/torque trace, reference action, and predicted residual, revealing when the residual policy modifies the reference during contact establishment, constrained motion, and recovery.

\subsubsection{Result analysis.}
We analyze the results along two dimensions. First, we compare gains on precision-contact tasks, such as USB and plug insertion, with those on articulated-motion tasks, such as cabinet-door and drawer opening. Second, we relate changes in peak force and completion time to the timing and magnitude of the predicted residual around contact events. This analysis determines where force-conditioned correction contributes beyond the reference policy.

\subsubsection{Failure analysis.}
We categorize failed trials into three groups: perception or task-selection errors inherited from the reference policy, contact deviations that exceed the range of continuous pose correction, and interactions that require a discrete strategy change. For each category, we report its frequency by method and illustrate a representative rollout. The first category tests the boundary of residual learning when the reference itself is incorrect; the latter two identify limitations of the additive correction model.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prediction target:} a residual policy supervised by $\left(A_k^*\right)^p-\left(A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}\right)^p$ and the proposed paired teacher residual.
    \item \textbf{Force representation:} instantaneous, history downsampled to the visual rate, and native-rate causal history.
    \item \textbf{Paired-query construction:} zero-force input, a learned null token, the learned token with the null adapter, and independent rather than shared flow noise.
\end{enumerate}
