We evaluate ForceDelta-VLA on single-arm and bimanual contact-rich manipulation. The experiments examine: (1) task performance and contact quality relative to external and architecture-matched baselines; (2) inference efficiency and robustness to delayed reference updates; and (3) the learned residual decomposition through diagnostics and ablations.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\noindent\textbf{Hardware, tasks, and data collection.}
We evaluate eight real-robot tasks---four single-arm and four bimanual---on two platforms (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). The single-arm platform is a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda with one wrist-mounted and one external Intel RealSense D405 camera; demonstrations are collected using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse. The bimanual platform is an X Square Robot with one wrist-mounted camera per arm and one shared external camera; demonstrations are collected through leader--follower teleoperation. Following ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, we use robot-provided wrench estimates; no additional wrist force/torque sensors are installed.

For each task, we collect $200$ demonstrations. Multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions are recorded at $30$~Hz, and timestamped wrench estimates at $100$~Hz.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and evaluation protocol.}
We compare against four external baselines: $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05visionlanguageactionmodelopenworld}, ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace}, and ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion}. We additionally roll out the Stage-1 teacher directly as Temporal ForceVLA, providing an architecture-matched comparison that isolates residual distillation. For each method and task, we evaluate two independently trained seeds with ten trials per seed and report success rate, peak contact force, and completion time.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
The temporal force-conditioned teacher is fully fine-tuned on eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs. All real-robot inference and runtime measurements use a single 48~GB NVIDIA RTX 4090D GPU.

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative and failure analysis.}
\assumed{On USB and plug insertion, the null-mode reference brings the connector to the target, after which the residual suppresses lateral motion and introduces small reorientation commands following asymmetric contact. During whiteboard wiping, the residual maintains surface contact despite changes in board pose. For cabinet and drawer opening, repeated corrections along the constrained direction compensate for hinge or rail friction without changing the reference strategy. Final failure counts will be grouped into incorrect references, deviations beyond the bounded residual range, and cases requiring a discrete recovery strategy.} The rollout examples will be illustrated by synchronized frames, force traces, references, and residuals. Incorrect references are inherited from the teacher, whereas the other failure modes expose the representational limit of an additive continuous correction. Cross-task claims about contact locality are reserved for the quantitative diagnostics in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_diagnostics}.

\noindent\textbf{Sensitivity to reference delay.}
We evaluate sensitivity to reference delay on USB insertion, plug insertion, and drawer opening by injecting query-completion delays of $0$--$300$~ms. Both methods continue executing their latest completed action chunk while a new query is pending. Temporal ForceVLA retains the previous full-action chunk, whereas ForceDelta-VLA retains the null-mode reference and continues updating its residual from current force/torque feedback.

\subsection{Residual Decomposition Analysis}
\label{sec:exp_diagnostics}

\noindent\textbf{Contact locality.}
To characterize the force-conditioned correction, we partition trajectories into free space, contact onset, and sustained contact using synchronized force thresholds and task-phase labels. For the teacher target $\Delta A_T^{\mathrm{force}}$ and student output $\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}$, we report the translational norm and the rotational change induced relative to the corresponding null-mode reference. The latter is computed as an $SO(3)$ geodesic angle after projecting the reference and force-corrected 6D representations to valid rotations.

\noindent\textbf{Reconstruction under reference staleness.}
We evaluate how closely the reference composed with both student outputs follows the current full-force teacher as the cached reference ages. Translation error is reported in physical units and rotation error as an $SO(3)$ geodesic angle after projection. Both are binned by cache age and compared with a student trained without the staleness target.
