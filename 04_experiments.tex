\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\noindent\textbf{Hardware, tasks, and data collection.}
The evaluation includes $5$ single-arm and $4$ bimanual tasks on two platforms (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). The single-arm platform is a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda with wrist-mounted and external Intel RealSense D405 cameras. Demonstrations are collected using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse. The bimanual platform is an X Square Robot with one wrist-mounted camera per arm and one shared external camera. Demonstrations are collected through leader--follower teleoperation. Following ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, both platforms use robot-provided wrench estimates without additional wrist force/torque sensors.

For each task, we collect $200$ demonstrations. Multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions are recorded at $30$~Hz, and timestamped wrench estimates at $100$~Hz.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and evaluation protocol.}
We compare against four external baselines: $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05visionlanguageactionmodelopenworld}, ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace}, and ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion}. Temporal Teacher directly rolls out the Stage-1 teacher and provides an architecture-matched comparison for residual distillation. For each method and task, we evaluate $2$ independently trained seeds with $10$ trials per seed. We report success rate, peak contact force, and completion time. Peak force is computed over all trials as $\max_t\|\mathbf f(t)\|_2$ for single-arm tasks and $\max_{t,a\in\{L,R\}}\|\mathbf f_a(t)\|_2$ for bimanual tasks. Completion time is computed over successful trials.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
Both platforms execute policy commands through Cartesian impedance controllers with proportional--derivative feedback. For safety, Cartesian commands are issued at a maximum rate of $100$~Hz on both platforms. The temporal force-conditioned teacher is fully fine-tuned using $8$ NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Real-robot deployment uses a workstation equipped with $2$ NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with 24~GB of memory each.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:exp_main}

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_button} shows a representative button-pressing rollout with synchronized front- and wrist-camera observations, robot-estimated end-effector force components, and translational residual magnitude. As the robot transitions into sustained contact, the residual increases together with the interaction force, exhibits a pronounced transient near peak contact, and decreases as the button is released. This behavior is consistent with the residual acting as a force-responsive local correction rather than an independently generated task trajectory.

\noindent\textbf{Task performance.}
ForceDelta-VLA achieves an average success rate of $82.2\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). The $\pi_{0.5}$, ForceVLA, and TA-VLA baselines reach $47.2\%$, $54.4\%$, and $57.2\%$, respectively. ImplicitRDP reaches $72.8\%$, and the architecture-matched Temporal Teacher reaches $68.9\%$. The largest gains over ImplicitRDP occur on USB insertion ($80\%$ vs. $55\%$), plug insertion ($70\%$ vs. $50\%$), and plug removal ($80\%$ vs. $65\%$). ForceDelta-VLA and ImplicitRDP obtain identical success rates on object flipping ($90\%$), button pressing ($85\%$), and single-arm whiteboard wiping ($90\%$).

Temporal force conditioning improves ForceVLA from $54.4\%$ to $68.9\%$, and ForceDelta-VLA further reaches $82.2\%$. Relative to the Temporal Teacher, ForceDelta-VLA reduces completion time by $19.2\%$ on single-arm tasks and $21.7\%$ on bimanual tasks. It is also slightly faster than ImplicitRDP on both platforms.

\noindent\textbf{Contact quality.}
ForceDelta-VLA yields the lowest peak contact force among all methods on both platforms (Table~\ref{tab:interaction}). Relative to the Temporal Teacher, peak force decreases from $15.3$ to $11.6$~N on single-arm tasks and from $16.1$ to $12.3$~N on bimanual tasks. These values correspond to reductions of $24.2\%$ and $23.6\%$. Relative to ImplicitRDP, the reductions are approximately $19\%$ on both platforms. The remaining VLA baselines exhibit higher peak forces.

\noindent\textbf{Failure analysis.}
Across the $180$ ForceDelta-VLA evaluation trials in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, we observe $32$ failures and group them into three categories. Of these, $14$ are \emph{reference failures}, in which the reference trajectory lies outside the corrective range of the bounded residual. Another $7$ result from \emph{excessive reference staleness}, with reference ages beyond the delay range used for training. The remaining $11$ require \emph{nonlocal recovery}, such as retreat, re-approach, or strategy switching after jamming or wrong-side contact.

\subsection{Independent Residual Execution}
\label{sec:exp_runtime}

Two experiments assess independent residual execution. Runtime profiling quantifies the realized rate separation between the reference and residual paths; reference-delay evaluation tests whether this separation improves contact behavior as the cached reference becomes stale.

\noindent\textbf{Realized rate separation.}
We profile all paths on the button-pressing task using the deployment hardware (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}). The ForceDelta residual requires $2.43$~ms per isolated forward pass, compared with $908.69$~ms for the null-mode reference. With the two workers assigned to separate GPUs, the residual publishes at $120.40$~Hz, while the reference updates at $1.06$~Hz. The $100$-Hz command interface applies the latest valid residual packet at each control update; packet publication rate is therefore distinct from command application frequency and the waypoint frequency within each packet. ImplicitRDP publishes reactive packets at $78.10$~Hz.

\noindent\textbf{Robustness to delayed references.}
We evaluate USB insertion and plug insertion with $0$, $100$, and $200$~ms of additional packet-publication delay, using $10$ trials per task, method, and delay. ForceDelta-VLA degrades more gradually than the Temporal Teacher: from $0$ to $200$~ms, success drops by $15$ versus $25$ percentage points, while peak contact force increases by approximately $30\%$ versus $60\%$. This suggests that independent residual updates improve robustness to delayed reference availability (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency_sensitivity}).
