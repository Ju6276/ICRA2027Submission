\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\noindent\textbf{Hardware, tasks, and data collection.}
The evaluation includes $5$ single-arm and $4$ bimanual tasks on two platforms (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). The single-arm platform is a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda with wrist-mounted and external Intel RealSense D405 cameras. Demonstrations are collected using a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse. The bimanual platform is an X Square Robot with one wrist-mounted camera per arm and one shared external camera. Demonstrations are collected through leader--follower teleoperation. Following ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, both platforms use robot-provided joint-torque-based wrench estimates without additional wrist force/torque sensors.

For each task, we collect $200$ demonstrations. Multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions are recorded at $30$~Hz, and timestamped wrench estimates at $100$~Hz.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and evaluation protocol.}
We select baselines to cover complementary comparisons. $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05visionlanguageactionmodelopenworld} represents a general force-agnostic VLA. ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, which also underlies our teacher, provides the direct force-aware full-action baseline. TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace} represents an alternative torque-aware VLA design. ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion} provides a strong end-to-end slow--fast visual--force comparison outside the VLA family. Temporal Teacher directly rolls out the Stage-1 teacher, controlling for temporal force conditioning when evaluating the complete residual framework. The architecture-matched Fast full-action distillation ablation in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_ablation} compares compact full-action generation with reference-anchored residual prediction. All methods use the same task demonstrations and are evaluated under the same initial-state distributions, controller settings, and safety limits. For each method and task, we conduct $20$ evaluation trials. We report success rate, peak contact force, and completion time. Peak force and completion time are computed over successful trials. For each successful trial, peak force is $\max_t\|\mathbf f(t)\|_2$ for single-arm tasks and $\max_{t,a\in\{L,R\}}\|\mathbf f_a(t)\|_2$ for bimanual tasks.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
Both platforms execute policy commands through Cartesian impedance controllers with proportional--derivative feedback. For safety, the Cartesian target-update rate is limited to $100$~Hz on both platforms. The temporal force-conditioned teacher is fully fine-tuned using $8$ NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Real-robot deployment uses a workstation equipped with $2$ NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with 24~GB of memory each. During ForceDelta-VLA deployment, the reference and residual workers run concurrently on separate GPUs, with one GPU dedicated to each worker.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:exp_main}

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_button} highlights how the learned translational correction evolves through contact. The correction is comparatively smaller before sustained contact, increases during pressing, exhibits a transient near peak contact, and decreases as contact is released. Its alignment with the force trace illustrates that the fast pathway concentrates its correction within the contact phase.

\noindent\textbf{Task performance.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} shows that ForceDelta-VLA reaches $82.2\%$ mean success, exceeding the strongest baseline, ImplicitRDP at $72.8\%$, by $9.4$ percentage points and the Temporal Teacher at $68.9\%$ by $13.3$ points. The remaining force-agnostic and full-action force-aware baselines stay below $60\%$. Across the nine tasks, the per-task margins over ImplicitRDP sum to $85$ percentage points. USB insertion contributes $25$ points, plug insertion $20$ points, and plug removal $15$ points; together, these three tasks account for $60$ points, or $70.6\%$ of the aggregate margin. ForceDelta-VLA and ImplicitRDP tie at $90\%$ on object flipping, $85\%$ on button pressing, and $90\%$ on single-arm whiteboard wiping.

The baseline progression distinguishes gains from temporal force modeling from those of the complete residual framework. Replacing ForceVLA's instantaneous force encoding with temporal force history improves the Temporal Teacher on all nine tasks, with a gain of $30$ percentage points on button pressing. Relative to the Temporal Teacher, the complete reference-anchored residual framework also improves all nine tasks, with gains of $25$ percentage points on USB insertion and $20$ percentage points on plug insertion. The component ablations below examine which parts of the framework contribute to these improvements.

ForceDelta-VLA also has the lowest mean successful-trial completion time on both platforms ($23.9$/$25.8$~s; Table~\ref{tab:interaction}). Thus, its higher success and lower contact force are not obtained through slower execution of successful trials.

\noindent\textbf{Contact quality.}
Among successful trials, ForceDelta-VLA yields the lowest platform-aggregated peak contact force among all methods (Table~\ref{tab:interaction}). Relative to the Temporal Teacher, peak force decreases from $15.1$ to $11.8$~N on single-arm tasks and from $14.7$ to $12.5$~N on bimanual tasks, reductions of $21.9\%$ and $15.0\%$. Relative to ImplicitRDP, the reductions are $15.7\%$ and $18.8\%$, respectively. The reduction is observed on both platforms alongside higher task success. Fast residual updates can limit subsequent commanded motion after a contact transient is observed, reducing the accumulation of excessive interaction force.

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative failure modes.}
Inspection of failed ForceDelta-VLA rollouts suggests three recurring limitations. First, an invalid reference can move the trajectory outside the bounded residual's correction envelope. Second, excessive reference age beyond the training schedule can make the cached local plan inconsistent with the evolving robot--environment state. Third, jamming or wrong-side contact can require nonlocal recovery---such as retreat, re-approach, or strategy switching---that cannot be expressed by a local pose correction.

\subsection{Independent Residual Execution}
\label{sec:exp_runtime}

Two experiments assess independent residual execution. Runtime profiling quantifies the inference-time separation between the reference and residual paths; reference-delay evaluation tests whether this separation improves contact behavior as the cached reference becomes stale.

\noindent\textbf{Inference-time separation.}
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} reports isolated model-forward latency under the same button-pressing deployment setting. The $100$-Hz Cartesian target-update limit corresponds to a $10$-ms model-inference budget for per-update evaluation. The ForceDelta residual forward pass takes $2.43$~ms and fits within this budget. The Temporal Teacher and ForceDelta reference take $176.4$ and $189.7$~ms, respectively, so these generative paths cannot be evaluated at every target update; ForceDelta instead computes its reference asynchronously and reuses it across residual updates.

\noindent\textbf{Robustness to delayed references.}
We evaluate USB insertion and plug insertion with $0$, $100$, and $200$~ms of additional reference-availability delay, using $10$ trials per task, method, and delay. Aggregated across the two tasks, ForceDelta-VLA maintains higher success and lower successful-trial peak force than the Temporal Teacher across the tested delays (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency_sensitivity}). From $0$ to $200$~ms, success falls from $75\%$ to $60\%$ for ForceDelta-VLA and from $60\%$ to $35\%$ for the Temporal Teacher, while successful-trial peak contact force rises from $12.5$ to $16.2$~N and from $16.0$ to $25.6$~N, respectively. On these two insertion tasks, ForceDelta-VLA degrades more gradually under additional reference delay.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

We organize the ablations into two groups (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
\emph{Execution/component ablations} keep the trained model fixed. \textbf{Reference only} executes the cached reference without learned corrections or state rebasing. \textbf{w/o force residual} disables the force output. \textbf{w/o learned staleness residual} disables the learned staleness output while retaining $\Gamma(S_t,S_k)$. \textbf{Shuffled force history} preserves the empirical force-history marginal but breaks its observation--action correspondence. \textbf{Zeroed residual input} replaces the measured force history with an all-zero tensor while retaining the force branch.

\emph{Supervision/decomposition ablations} retrain the affected variant. \textbf{Demonstration-derived residual target} subtracts the missing-force teacher prediction from the demonstrated action at the same query state. \textbf{Zero-wrench teacher query} replaces the learned missing-force mode during reference and target construction with an all-zero-wrench teacher query. \textbf{w/o schedule replay} omits the sampled asynchronous schedule used for cached-context target extraction. \textbf{Single combined-residual head} predicts the sum of the two learned targets with one head while retaining deterministic rebasing. \textbf{Fast full-action distillation} uses the same compact student inputs and update schedule but directly distills the frozen teacher's force-conditioned pose action.

Shuffling the force history produces the largest degradation among the execution variants, exceeding the effect of zeroing the residual force input. Because shuffling preserves the marginal force magnitude while breaking its correspondence with the current interaction state, this contrast shows that the residual policy depends on correctly paired force observations. Removing the force residual also causes a larger degradation than removing the learned staleness residual on both platforms, indicating that the force residual is the dominant learned component in the evaluated tasks, while the staleness residual provides an additional improvement.

The supervision ablations favor the proposed target construction and output decomposition. The separated force-conditioned and force-masked residual design outperforms the single predictor trained on the summed target, and schedule replay improves both success and peak force. Direct compact full-action distillation performs worse than reference-anchored residual prediction, while demonstration-derived targets and the zero-wrench teacher query also degrade both metrics. Together, these comparisons support paired teacher queries, decomposed residual prediction, and reference-anchored execution as complementary parts of the full model.
