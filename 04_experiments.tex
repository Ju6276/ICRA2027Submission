\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\subsubsection{Hardware platforms.}
The single-arm platform consists of a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda manipulator observed by two Intel RealSense D405 cameras. We use a separate bimanual platform for the coordinated tasks described below.

\subsubsection{Tasks and data collection.}
We evaluate button pressing, object flipping, USB insertion, and kitchen-door opening with the single-arm platform, and plug insertion, plug removal, whiteboard wiping, and drawer opening with the bimanual platform (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). We collect $200$ and $300$ teleoperated demonstrations per single-arm and bimanual task, respectively. Each trajectory records synchronized multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions at $30$~Hz, together with timestamped six-axis force/torque measurements at $100$~Hz.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_baselines}

We compare against four published policies. $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05} is a vision--language policy without force input. ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla} fuses instantaneous force through a force-aware mixture of experts, and TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavla} conditions its decoder on torque history. ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2025implicitrdp} is a visual--force diffusion policy with structural slow--fast processing.

The primary metric is task success rate. For each method and task, we evaluate two training seeds with ten trials per seed, and report per-task success together with the mean over the four single-arm and the four bimanual tasks separately. We additionally report two interaction metrics over successful trials: the peak contact force, taken as the maximum force magnitude recorded during the trial, and the task completion time. Both are averaged per task group.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp_implementation}

All models are trained on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs using bfloat16 precision. We fully fine-tune the teacher for $50{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $32$, a peak learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, $1{,}000$ warmup steps, cosine decay, and gradient clipping at $1.0$. The teacher predicts $H{=}50$ actions at $30$~Hz and encodes the latest $100$~ms of the $100$~Hz force/torque stream. Flow-matching inference uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ steps.

We freeze the teacher and jointly optimize the null token and rank-$32$ null-specific adapter for $10{,}000$ steps with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. The visual--language prefix is pooled into $M{=}16$ tokens. The residual student uses one pre-norm attention block and predicts $K{=}5$ corrections. It is trained for $30{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $128$, and a learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$. We set $\lambda_{\mathrm{res}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{comp}}=1$ and $\beta=0.5$.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prediction target:} a cached-prefix fast branch that regenerates complete actions, a residual student trained directly on demonstrated actions, and the proposed student trained on paired counterfactual residual targets.
    \item \textbf{Force representation:} instantaneous, history downsampled to the visual rate, and native-rate causal history.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual construction:} zero-force input, a learned null token, the learned token with the null adapter, and independent rather than shared flow noise.
\end{enumerate}
