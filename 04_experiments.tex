We evaluate ForceDelta-VLA in terms of task performance, contact quality, responsiveness to delayed references, and the contribution of its main design components.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\subsubsection{Hardware platforms.}
Experiments use a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda with one wrist-mounted and one external Intel RealSense D405 camera, and an X Square Robot bimanual platform with one wrist-mounted camera per arm and one shared external camera. Following ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, each arm receives the robot-estimated external end-effector wrench $\mathbf w=[\mathbf f;\boldsymbol\tau]\in\mathbb R^6$ in its base frame; no additional wrist force/torque sensor is used.

\subsubsection{Tasks and data collection.}
We evaluate four single-arm and four bimanual tasks (Table~\ref{tab:tasks}), collecting $200$ and $300$ demonstrations per task, respectively. Single-arm demonstrations are collected with a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse, whereas bimanual demonstrations use leader--follower teleoperation on the X Square Robot platform. Multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions are recorded at $30$~Hz, and timestamped wrench estimates at $100$~Hz.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_baselines}

We compare against four external baselines: $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05visionlanguageactionmodelopenworld}, ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels}, TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace}, and ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion}. We additionally include an architecture-matched Temporal ForceVLA baseline that directly executes the Stage-1 teacher with the same temporal force encoder and weights, but without null-mode residual distillation, isolating the contribution of residual decomposition. For each method and task, we evaluate two independently trained seeds with ten trials per seed and report success rate, peak contact force, and completion time.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp_implementation}

Training uses two NVIDIA A100 GPUs, while all real-robot inference and runtime measurements use a single NVIDIA H20 GPU. \assumed{The temporal force-conditioned teacher is fine-tuned for $50{,}000$ steps with AdamW at a peak learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$, predicts $H{=}50$ actions at $30$~Hz, and encodes the latest $100$~ms of the $100$~Hz wrench stream; flow-matching inference uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ steps.}

After freezing the teacher, \assumed{we train the rank-$32$ null-mode adapter for $10{,}000$ steps at $10^{-4}$. The visual--language prefix is pooled into $M{=}16$ intent tokens, and the residual policy uses a causal force encoder with one pre-norm attention block and separate force and staleness heads to predict $K{=}5$ corrections. It is trained for $30{,}000$ steps at $3\times10^{-4}$ under replayed asynchronous schedules.}

Actions contain 3D translation, a continuous 6D rotation representation~\cite{zhou2020continuityrotationrepresentationsneural}, and a scalar gripper command per arm. At deployment, the force-conditioned and staleness corrections are independently bounded in the normalized pose-action space before being composed with the reference action.

\subsubsection{Qualitative and failure analysis.}
\assumed{On USB and plug insertion, the null-mode reference brings the connector to the target, after which the residual suppresses lateral motion and introduces small reorientation commands following asymmetric contact. During whiteboard wiping, the residual maintains surface contact despite changes in board pose. For cabinet and drawer opening, repeated corrections along the constrained direction compensate for hinge or rail friction without changing the reference strategy. Final failure counts will be grouped into incorrect references, deviations beyond the bounded residual range, and cases requiring a discrete recovery strategy.} The rollout examples will be illustrated by synchronized frames, force traces, references, and residuals. Incorrect references are inherited from the teacher, whereas the other failure modes expose the representational limit of an additive continuous correction. Cross-task claims about contact locality are reserved for the quantitative diagnostics in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Residual Diagnostics}
\label{sec:exp_diagnostics}

We test whether the distilled residual represents contact-dependent correction rather than a second complete-action policy. Trajectories are divided into free space, contact onset, and sustained contact using synchronized force thresholds and task phase labels. We report translation magnitude and rotation geodesic magnitude of $\Delta A_T^{\mathrm{force}}$ in each phase, together with the same quantities for the student prediction.

We additionally measure how accurately the composed student reconstructs the current full-force teacher,
\begin{align}
    e_{\mathrm{rec}}(t)=d\big(&\mathcal P(A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{full}}), \nonumber\\[-2pt]
      &\mathcal P(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t))
       +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}(t)
       +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}(t)\big),
    \label{eq:reconstruction_error}
\end{align}
where translation error is reported in physical units and rotation error as an $SO(3)$ geodesic angle after projection. Errors are binned by cache age and compared with a student trained without the staleness target. This analysis directly tests whether staleness supervision maintains teacher reconstruction as the cached reference ages.
