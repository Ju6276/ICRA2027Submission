\subsection{Experimental Objectives}
\label{sec:exp_objectives}

The evaluation addresses four questions. First, does supervising the high-rate branch with the force-attributable deviation rather than the full action change the attainable control rate? Second, does the deviation carry sufficient structure for a small student to learn, and does the student rely on force rather than inferring corrections from context? Third, does execution at the sensor rate improve contact outcomes relative to the rate a cached-prefix action expert attains? Fourth, does randomized-timing training permit a single student to transfer across deployment rates?

\subsection{Attainable Rate of the High-Rate Path}
\label{sec:exp_latency}

The per-update cost of the two designs is measured on the same device at batch size one. For the cached-prefix baseline the vision--language key/value cache is supplied directly and one action-expert forward pass is timed over $816$ prefix and $51$ suffix tokens, then scaled by the sampler's denoising step count; the vision--language expert is replaced by a narrow placeholder that is never executed, so the measurement isolates the incremental cost that caching leaves behind. For the student, one complete forward pass is timed, including the force encoder and the intent projector.

Table~\ref{tab:latency} reports a $31.8\times$ reduction at the standard $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ setting. Two aspects are more informative than the ratio itself. The $13$~Hz figure agrees with the $15$~Hz reported by ForceVLA2 on the same GPU class~\cite{li2026forcevla2}, indicating that the measurement reflects the design rather than the harness. Furthermore, a single action-expert forward pass already consumes $76\%$ of a $10$~ms budget, so the constraint is not a step-count choice that can be tuned away; attaining $100$~Hz along that path would require one-step flow matching, which is not an established sampler for this policy class. The student meets the same budget with a factor of four in reserve.

\subsection{Tasks and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

Evaluation covers contact-rich manipulation tasks spanning four interaction regimes: contact establishment, constrained motion along a surface or mechanism, precision insertion, and recovery from externally induced disturbance. At least one task requires force closure, so that a vision-only policy fails outright, this being the regime in which a force-attributable correction is expected to be decisive. For every task, demonstrations contain synchronized multi-view images, language instructions, robot states, commanded actions, and timestamped six-axis force/torque measurements retained at their native rate.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_baselines}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vision--language policy:} no force input and no high-rate branch.
    \item \textbf{Instantaneous-force teacher:} force sampled only at the visual frame, isolating the value of native-rate history.
    \item \textbf{Temporal force-aware teacher:} our teacher executed as a monolithic policy at its attainable rate, providing the accuracy ceiling of the distillation and the rate floor of the comparison.
    \item \textbf{Cached-prefix fast branch:} the teacher's action expert re-run against the cached prefix in the manner of FAVLA and LIFT~\cite{li2026favla,lv2026lift}, executed at the rate permitted by Table~\ref{tab:latency}. This is the primary point of comparison.
    \item \textbf{Direct slow--fast behavior cloning:} the student architecture trained from demonstrated actions alone, without paired teacher targets, isolating the supervision from the architecture.
    \item \textbf{FoRe-VLA:} the full method.
\end{enumerate}

Task metrics are success rate and stage-wise completion. Interaction quality is measured by peak force, force impulse, limit violations, and post-disturbance recovery rate. Decomposition quality is measured by the residual error $d(\Delta \hat{A},\Delta A_T^F)$ and the composed error $d(A^{\mathrm{ref}}{+}\Delta \hat{A},A_T^{\mathrm{full}})$, each relative to a zero-residual baseline so that the reported quantity is the fraction of the deviation captured. Errors are computed in physical units on the reconstructed absolute pose rather than on normalized deltas, and reported separately for translation and rotation. Runtime metrics are the slow rate, the fast rate, end-to-end latency, and parameter count.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp_implementation}

The teacher is a $\pi_0$-style ForceVLA with LoRA adapters on both the vision--language backbone and the action expert, an action horizon of $H{=}50$ at a $30$~Hz action rate. Its causal TCN force encoder reads a $100$~ms window from the timestamped stream at $100$~Hz, rejecting samples older than $12$~ms. Null adaptation uses rank $\rho{=}32$ on the $9$ pose channels. Target extraction uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ and pools the prefix into $M{=}16$ bins of width $2048$.

The student uses one attention block of width $1024$, MLP dimension $4096$, $8$ query heads over $1$ key/value head, head dimension $256$, intent width $1024$, and emits $K{=}5$ steps with step decay $\beta{=}0.5$; its force encoder reads the same $100$~ms window at $100$~Hz. Randomized timing draws the slow rate from $[5,15]$~Hz, the inference latency from $[50,300]$~ms, and per-update jitter from $\pm10$~ms, with age scale $c{=}0.6$~s chosen to cover that band without clipping. Demonstrations are restricted to episodes whose force stream sustains at least $100$~Hz.

\subsection{Execution Rate}
\label{sec:exp_rate}

The trained student is held fixed and its execution rate capped at $13$, $30$, $50$, and $100$~Hz, the first three matching the rates that Table~\ref{tab:latency} permits for a cached-prefix action expert, and peak contact force, impulse, and success rate are measured at each. Matched contact events are additionally replayed under three configurations, namely the slow branch alone, the slow and fast branches synchronized at the slow rate, and full asynchronous execution. For each we report the delay from a detected force change to the first corrective command, the maximum force preceding recovery, and the time to return to the nominal trajectory. This isolates the benefit attributable to rate from the benefit attributable to the residual itself: the latency reduction of Table~\ref{tab:latency} is consequential only insofar as contact outcomes improve with rate.

\subsection{Analysis of the Counterfactual Decomposition}
\label{sec:exp_decomposition}

We inspect the paired teacher predictions used as targets. For each trajectory, $\lVert\Delta A_T^F\rVert$ is plotted against measured force and task phase; a useful decomposition exhibits small deviations during unconstrained motion and larger, structured deviations near contact onset, transients, and recovery.

Three quantities characterize the decomposition. The first is the relative magnitude of the force-attributable signal, $\sigma_F^2/\sigma_R^2$, where $\sigma_F^2 = \mathbb{E}\lVert\Delta A_T^F\rVert^2$ and $\sigma_R^2 = \mathbb{E}\lVert A^{\mathrm{ref}} - A_T^{\mathrm{null}}\rVert^2$ is the reference error incurred by staleness alone. A ratio near or above unity indicates that the force-attributable component is comparable to the staleness error and thus warrants a dedicated branch, whereas a small ratio would indicate that the composed error is dominated by staleness and that increasing the slow rate is the more effective intervention. The second is the student's dependence on force, obtained by comparing its residual-error reduction against that of a force-ablated student; a small gap would indicate that corrections are being inferred from intent, state, and reference rather than from force. The third is the locality of that gap, reported separately for contact strata and free space, since a correction that is genuinely contact-driven should exhibit a larger margin under contact.

We further compare the learned null condition against two alternatives used in prior work, a zero force vector as in zero-padding interventions~\cite{idr2026causal} and random force-token masking, testing the claim of Sec.~\ref{sec:null_decomp} that a learned null mode keeps the teacher in distribution. The teacher-to-demonstration gap $d(A_T^{\mathrm{full}},A^{\mathrm{expert}})$ is reported by translation, rotation, and gripper, as it bounds the accuracy attainable by any student distilled from this teacher.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Force representation:} instantaneous, history downsampled to the visual rate, and native-rate causal history.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual construction:} learned null condition, zero-force padding, learned token without the rank-$\rho$ adapter, and independent rather than shared flow noise, the last isolating the claim of Sec.~\ref{sec:teacher_differencing} that noise coupling is necessary for the target to be meaningful.
    \item \textbf{Student context:} removing cached intent, state, the reference token, the time token, or the multi-token force sequence.
    \item \textbf{Timing scheme:} fixed slow rate and latency versus randomized, evaluated at both the trained operating point and unseen rates, to test rate transfer.
    \item \textbf{Chunk emission:} $K{=}1$ versus $K{=}5$ under injected inference stalls.
\end{enumerate}
