Our experiments address task performance, contact quality, system responsiveness, and component contributions. Specifically, we ask whether ForceDelta-VLA improves success over force-free, force-aware, and fast--slow policies; whether the gain remains relative to the exact temporal force-conditioned teacher from which it is distilled; whether the residual improves physical interaction rather than producing more aggressive motion; how performance changes with reference delay and residual constraints; and which components of the proposed distillation account for the improvement.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

\subsubsection{Hardware platforms.}
We evaluate on a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda and a bimanual platform from X Square Robot. The single-arm setup uses one wrist-mounted and one external-view Intel RealSense D405 camera; the bimanual setup uses one wrist-mounted camera per arm and a shared external-view camera. Force feedback is obtained from the six-axis Cartesian wrench estimates provided by the robots, with one wrench stream per arm and no additional wrist-mounted force/torque sensors.

\subsubsection{Tasks and data collection.}
We evaluate the eight tasks in Table~\ref{tab:tasks}. Each task emphasizes a distinct contact regime rather than language or object generalization. We collect $200$ teleoperated demonstrations per single-arm task and $300$ per bimanual task. Multi-view images, robot states, and commanded actions are synchronized at $30$~Hz; timestamped robot-estimated external-wrench signals are recorded at $100$~Hz.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
\label{sec:exp_baselines}

We compare with five representative policies. The external baselines are the force-free $\pi_{0.5}$ VLA~\cite{black2025pi05}; ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla}, which fuses instantaneous force through a force-aware mixture of experts; TA-VLA~\cite{zhang2025tavla}, which injects temporal torque features into a pretrained VLA; and ImplicitRDP~\cite{chen2025implicitrdp}, a publicly reproducible fast--slow visual--force diffusion policy. ImplicitRDP serves as a fast--slow visual--force baseline rather than an asynchronous VLA baseline. Our architecture-matched baseline, \emph{Temporal ForceVLA}, directly rolls out the Stage-1 temporal force-conditioned teacher. It uses the same causal force encoder and teacher weights as ForceDelta-VLA, but neither learns a null mode nor distills a residual policy. This comparison isolates the contribution of residual decomposition from that of temporal force conditioning.

For each method and task, we evaluate two independently trained seeds with ten trials per seed. The primary metric is success rate, reported per task and averaged separately over the four single-arm and four bimanual tasks. We accompany pooled success rates with Wilson $95\%$ confidence intervals and provide the two seed-wise success counts in the supplementary material. Contact quality is measured over all trials by peak contact force and force impulse, defined as the time integral of force magnitude until success or termination. Completion time is reported over successful trials. Continuous metrics are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation; lower values indicate less severe contact, lower accumulated interaction load, and faster successful execution, respectively.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:exp_implementation}

All models are trained on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs using bfloat16 precision. \assumed{The teacher is fully fine-tuned for $50{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $32$, a peak learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, $1{,}000$ warmup steps, cosine decay, and gradient clipping at $1.0$.} The teacher predicts $H{=}50$ actions at $30$~Hz and encodes the latest $100$~ms of the $100$~Hz force/torque stream. \assumed{Flow-matching inference uses $N_{\mathrm{flow}}{=}10$ steps.}

Each arm is controlled by a pose displacement comprising 3D translation and a continuous 6D rotation representation~\cite{zhou2019continuity}, together with a scalar gripper command; bimanual actions concatenate the commands of both arms. Robot states and actions are normalized per channel. The displacement map $\Gamma$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:staleness_target} follows the same pose convention and converts state displacement to normalized action coordinates using the training-set statistics.

After freezing the teacher, \assumed{we optimize the null token and rank-$32$ null-specific adapter for $10{,}000$ steps at a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. The visual--language prefix is pooled into $M{=}16$ tokens. The residual policy contains a causal force encoder and one pre-norm attention block with separate force and staleness output projections. It predicts $K{=}5$ corrections and is trained for $30{,}000$ steps with AdamW, a global batch size of $128$, a learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$, and $\beta{=}0.5$.} During student training, the cached-reference age is sampled by replaying the asynchronous schedule described in Sec.~\ref{sec:method}.

At deployment, translation and rotation corrections are clipped separately at every residual update. \assumed{The final bounds are $X$~mm and $X^{\circ}$ per predicted step.} For bimanual policies, clipping is applied independently to each arm before the corrections are composed with the reference.

\subsubsection{Qualitative analysis.}
\assumed{On USB and plug insertion, the null-mode reference brings the connector to the target, after which the residual suppresses lateral motion and introduces small reorientation commands following asymmetric contact. During whiteboard wiping, the residual maintains surface contact despite changes in board pose. For cabinet and drawer opening, repeated corrections along the constrained direction compensate for hinge or rail friction without changing the reference strategy. In free-space segments, the predicted force-conditioned correction remains close to zero.} These observations will be illustrated by synchronized rollout frames, force traces, references, and residuals in the final qualitative figure.

\subsubsection{Failure analysis.}
\assumed{Final failure counts will be divided into incorrect references, deviations beyond the bounded residual range, and failures requiring a discrete recovery strategy.} The first category is inherited from the teacher, whereas the latter categories expose the representational limit of an additive continuous correction.

\subsection{Residual Diagnostics}
\label{sec:exp_diagnostics}

We test whether the distilled branch represents contact-dependent correction rather than a second complete-action policy. Trajectories are divided into free space, contact onset, and sustained contact using synchronized force thresholds and task phase labels. We report translation magnitude and rotation geodesic magnitude of $\Delta A_T^{\mathrm{force}}$ in each phase, together with the same quantities for the student prediction.

We additionally measure how accurately the composed student reconstructs the current full-force teacher,
\begin{align}
    e_{\mathrm{rec}}(t)=d\big(&\mathcal P(A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{full}}), \nonumber\\[-2pt]
      &\mathcal P(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t))
       +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}(t)
       +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}(t)\big),
    \label{eq:reconstruction_error}
\end{align}
where translation error is reported in physical units and rotation error as an $SO(3)$ geodesic angle after projection. Errors are binned by cache age and compared with a student trained without the staleness target. This analysis directly tests whether staleness supervision maintains teacher reconstruction as the cached reference ages.
