\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

We are given a dataset $\mathcal{D}$ of teleoperated contact-rich manipulation trajectories. At time $t$, each trajectory provides visual observations $V_t$, a language instruction $L$, robot state $S_t$, a timestamped force/torque history $\mathcal{F}_t$, and the demonstrated action $A_t^*$. An action is represented as
\begin{equation}
    A=[\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\;r\in\mathbb{R}^{6};\;g\in\mathbb{R}\,]
    \in\mathbb{R}^{10},
    \label{eq:action_space}
\end{equation}
where $p$ is translation, $r$ is a continuous 6D rotation representation~\cite{zhou2019continuity}, and $g$ is the gripper command. We write $A^p=[p;r]\in\mathbb{R}^{9}$ for the pose component. Pose channels are displacements from the robot state that conditioned the prediction, so a chunk is interpretable only together with that base state. Actions and states are normalized by their per-channel training statistics, and $\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_S/\sigma_A)$ expresses a state displacement in action units.

Let $T_\theta$ denote a force-conditioned VLA trained on $\mathcal{D}$ to predict an $H$-step action chunk. Evaluating $T_\theta$ at the rate contact requires is not possible, since a flow-matching query over a visual--language backbone costs tens of milliseconds. We therefore split the policy into a reference $A^{\mathrm{ref}}$ that is refreshed only when a teacher query completes, and a compact residual policy $\pi_\psi$ that runs at the control rate.

Let $t_k$ be the request time of the most recent completed query and $S_k$ the state it was conditioned on. At a control instant $t>t_k$ the residual policy produces two pose corrections in a single pass,
\begin{equation}
    \left(\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}(t),\,
          \Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}(t)\right)
    =\pi_\psi\!\left(\mathcal{F}_t,S_t,A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t),
      Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}},\xi_{t,k}\right),
    \label{eq:residual_policy}
\end{equation}
where $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}$ is the cached task context of query $k$ and $\xi_{t,k}$ encodes the timing relation between $t$ and that query. The executed command is
\begin{equation}
    (A^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t))^p
    =(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t))^p
     +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}(t)
     +\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}(t),
    \qquad
    g^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)=g^{\mathrm{ref}}(t).
    \label{eq:action_comp}
\end{equation}
Two corrections appear because the reference is out of date in two unrelated senses. It was computed from an observation at $t_k$, without the contact measured since, and $\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}$ accounts for that; its pose channels are also displacements from $S_k$ rather than from the current $S_t$, which is what $\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}$ resolves. The gripper is left to the reference, being a task-level decision rather than a contact-driven quantity.

The demonstrations supervise only $A_t^*$ and label neither correction. The learning problem is to construct both targets from $\mathcal{D}$ and the trained teacher, and to fit $\pi_\psi$ without task rewards or corrective demonstrations. ForceDelta-VLA does so in three stages, summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:network_structure}: we train a temporal force-conditioned teacher, freeze it and learn a null-force mode, then freeze both modes and distill paired targets into $\pi_\psi$. Only the last stage trains a student; the reference comes from the frozen teacher throughout.

\subsection{Temporal Force-Conditioned Teacher}
\label{sec:teacher}

We build the teacher on ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla}, replacing its instantaneous force input with a causal temporal encoder. At an inference instant $t$, a causal temporal convolutional network consumes a fixed-duration window of the native-rate force stream together with a validity mask for samples unavailable near the start of a trajectory,
\begin{equation}
    z_t^F=E_T^F\!\left(\{F(\tau)\mid t-w\leq\tau\leq t\}\right),
    \label{eq:teacher_tcn}
\end{equation}
and its output replaces the original force embedding in the multimodal fusion module. The remaining action-generation architecture is unchanged. The teacher is fully fine-tuned on the demonstrations to predict
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{full}}
    =T_\theta(V_t,L,S_t,z_t^F;\epsilon)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{H\times10},
    \label{eq:full_teacher}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is the initial noise of the flow-matching action generator. After this stage, $\theta$ is fixed.

\subsection{Learning the Null-Force Reference}
\label{sec:null_decomp}

The residual target should describe how the force-conditioned teacher changes its prediction relative to a valid reference policy. Simply setting force to zero is unsuitable: zero is a physically meaningful measurement and does not denote an unavailable force condition. We instead learn a null-force mode and compare it with the factual-force mode under matched sampling conditions.

The null mode comprises a learned token $z_{\varnothing F}$ and a lightweight null-specific adapter, trained jointly in the same stage. The token represents the absence of an instance-specific force history, while the adapter restores action prediction under that condition. With $\phi=\{z_{\varnothing F},W_{\mathrm{in}},W_{\mathrm{out}}\}$, the rank-$\rho$ adapter modifies only the pose channels of the action expert,
\begin{equation}
    v^p\leftarrow v^p+W_{\mathrm{out}}\,
    \sigma(W_{\mathrm{in}}h),
    \label{eq:nominal_adapter}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the action-expert hidden state. Holding $\theta$ fixed, $\phi$ is optimized with the teacher's flow-matching objective,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{null}}
    =\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D},\tau,\epsilon}
      \left[\ell_{\mathrm{FM}}\!\left(
      T_{\theta,\phi}^{\mathrm{null}},
      A_{t:t+H-1}^{*};\tau,\epsilon\right)\right],
    \label{eq:null_training}
\end{equation}
giving the null-mode prediction
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{null}}
    =T_{\theta,\phi}^{\mathrm{null}}
      (V_t,L,S_t,z_{\varnothing F};\epsilon).
    \label{eq:teacher_null}
\end{equation}
The adapter is enabled only in the null mode; the factual-force mode retains the trained teacher parameters and computation. This prediction is the slowly updated reference at deployment.

\subsection{Counterfactual Residual Targets}
\label{sec:teacher_differencing}

Because the teacher uses flow matching, independently sampled predictions differ for reasons unrelated to the conditioning mode. For each training sample we therefore reuse the visual--language prefix across the two modes and initialize both with the same noise $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. The two predictions are then displacements from a common base state $S_t$, and their difference isolates the conditioning mode,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{force}}
    =\left(A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{full}}\right)^p
      -\left(A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{null}}\right)^p
      \in\mathbb{R}^{H\times9}.
    \label{eq:force_target}
\end{equation}
We call this a counterfactual residual because it compares two operating modes of one teacher under matched observations and sampling noise. It is a model-level conditional difference rather than a causal attribution of contact: the adapted null mode differs parametrically from the factual mode, and vision and state may themselves encode contact.

The second target concerns the reference the deployed system actually holds at $t$, which comes from query $k$ and is anchored at $S_k$. Bringing it onto the base state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher_null} gives
\begin{equation}
    \Delta A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{stale}}
    =\left(A_{T,t}^{\mathrm{null}}\right)^p
      -\left(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\right)^p
      +\Lambda\left(S_t-S_k\right)^p .
    \label{eq:staleness_target}
\end{equation}
Without the last term the expression would subtract two displacements measured from different origins. The first two terms are the drift of the teacher's own nominal prediction over the interval, and the third is the motion the arm completed while the query was in flight. Summing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:force_target} and~\eqref{eq:staleness_target} recovers the gap between the held reference and the factual-force teacher at $t$, which is the correction Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_comp} requires.

\subsection{Fast Residual Policy}
\label{sec:residual_policy}

Each completed query publishes the reference chunk together with a compact task representation. Given the visual--language prefix $E_k\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$ and its mask $m_k$, we pool contiguous token groups into $M$ intent tokens,
\begin{equation}
    Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}
    =\mathrm{Pool}_M(E_k,m_k)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d},
    \label{eq:intent_projection}
\end{equation}
which lets the residual policy reuse the teacher's task context without a second visual--language backbone. A causal TCN encodes the latest force window into a single token $Z_t^F$. Because both corrections depend on how much time has elapsed since the active query, the policy is also conditioned on
\begin{equation}
    \xi_{t,k}=\left[\;\min\!\left(\tfrac{t-t_k}{c},1\right);\;\alpha_{t,k}\;\right],
    \label{eq:time_features}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is a fixed age scale and $\alpha_{t,k}$ is the position of $t$ between the two reference samples that bracket it. The first entry reports the age of the cached context, the second the phase within a reference interval.

The conditioning set is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{C}_t=\left\{\widetilde Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}},\;
      Z_t^F,\;P_S(S_t),\;P_A(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)),\;P_\xi(\xi_{t,k})\right\},
    \label{eq:conditioning_set}
\end{equation}
with learned projections $P_S,P_A,P_\xi$ and additive type embeddings. A compact attention block $\Phi$ reads out at a learned query $q$, and the two corrections are taken from two passes over the same block,
\begin{align}
    \Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}_{t,1:K}
    &=W_{\mathrm{force}}\,
      \Phi\!\left(\mathcal{C}_t\cup\{q\}\right)[q],
    \label{eq:force_output}\\
    \Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}_{t,1:K}
    &=W_{\mathrm{stale}}\,
      \Phi\!\left((\mathcal{C}_t\setminus\{Z_t^F\})\cup\{q\}\right)[q],
    \label{eq:staleness_output}
\end{align}
both in $\mathbb{R}^{K\times9}$. The target in Eq.~\eqref{eq:staleness_target} is a difference between force-free quantities, and withholding the force token makes the corresponding output independent of the measured force by construction. Because the conditions attend to one another, the second pass recomputes the block rather than reusing representations that have already absorbed force. The two passes share all parameters except $W_{\mathrm{force}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{stale}}$, both initialized to zero, so an untrained policy leaves the reference unchanged.

Each pass emits $K$ steps rather than one, all conditioned on force and state only up to $t$, in the same way that the teacher forms its whole chunk from a single observation. The additional steps cover the interval until the next residual update, so the worker need not complete within one control period and its rate need not be fixed in advance.

\subsection{Asynchronous Distillation}
\label{sec:async_training}

A sample drawn at one instant would pair task context, force, state and reference from the same time, whereas the deployed policy always combines a cached context with newer force and state. We therefore simulate the deployment schedule offline: for a chosen slow rate and query latency, we identify for every control instant $t$ the query $k$ that would then be the most recent completed one, and materialize its reference samples and timing features. The intent tokens in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditioning_set} are taken from $k$, the remaining entries from $t$, and the targets of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:force_target} and~\eqref{eq:staleness_target} are evaluated at $t$. Since both teacher modes are frozen, the schedule is applied to precomputed teacher outputs and adds no backbone evaluations.

A fixed schedule would expose the policy to a single operating point. We resample the slow rate and the query latency at intervals during training, so the context age varies across a range and Eq.~\eqref{eq:time_features} carries the information needed to tell those conditions apart.

The policy is trained on the first $K$ steps of both targets. With a pose-space regression loss $d$, a weight $\lambda$ on the staleness term, and normalized step weights $w_j\propto\beta^{j-1}$ with $\beta<1$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =\sum_{j=1}^{K}w_j\left[
      d\!\left(\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}_{t,j},
      \Delta A^{\mathrm{force}}_{T,t,j}\right)
      +\lambda\,
      d\!\left(\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}_{t,j},
      \Delta A^{\mathrm{stale}}_{T,t,j}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:res_loss}
\end{equation}
so that steps executed earlier in the chunk carry more weight. Each term is supervised on its own output rather than through their sum.

\subsection{Asynchronous Deployment}
\label{sec:execution}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig03_async_deployment.pdf}
    \caption{Asynchronous deployment. The reference and residual workers execute independently. A completed VLA query atomically replaces the timestamped cache; residual updates continue against the latest complete cache while the next VLA query is running.}
    \label{fig:async_deployment}
\end{figure}

Deployment runs two independent workers, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:async_deployment}. At request time $t_k$ the reference worker snapshots $(V_k,L,S_k)$ and invokes the frozen teacher in its null-force mode. On completion at $\bar t_k$ it atomically publishes a versioned packet holding $A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}$, $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}$, the action timestamps, and the base state $S_k$. Publishing the packet as one unit keeps the residual worker from pairing a reference chunk with intent tokens from a different query, and carrying $S_k$ makes the base state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:staleness_target} available at run time rather than inferred.

Reference samples are timestamped relative to the request time at the action rate $f_A$,
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{k,j}=t_k+(j-1)/f_A,\qquad j=1,\ldots,H,
    \label{eq:reference_timestamps}
\end{equation}
and those with $\tau_{k,j}<\bar t_k$ are expired on publication. At a control instant $t$, $A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ is interpolated from the valid samples bracketing $t$, and the previous packet stays active until a newer complete one arrives. Execution therefore resumes at the sample matching the current time instead of at the head of a chunk that was formed before the query returned, and the same timestamps define $\xi_{t,k}$.

The residual worker follows its own schedule and never waits. At each update it snapshots the latest packet, reads $\mathcal{F}_t$ and $S_t$, and evaluates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:force_output} and~\eqref{eq:staleness_output} in one deterministic pass. Writing $j(t)$ for the emitted step whose timestamp matches $t$, the executed form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_comp} applies a separate bound to each correction,
\begin{equation}
    (A^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t))^p
    =(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t))^p
     +\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{force}}_{t,j(t)},\kappa_F\right)
     +\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\Delta\hat A^{\mathrm{stale}}_{t,j(t)},\kappa_S\right),
    \label{eq:deploy_composition}
\end{equation}
after which the command is unnormalized and added to $S_k$ to obtain an absolute pose. The bounds differ because $\kappa_F$ limits how far measured contact may depart from the planned motion, whereas the staleness correction advances the cached plan to the present and is routinely the larger of the two.
