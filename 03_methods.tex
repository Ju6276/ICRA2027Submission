\subsection{Problem Formulation and Overview}
\label{sec:problem}

We study contact-rich manipulation in which a policy observes images $V$, a language instruction $L$, robot state $S$, and a timestamped stream of force/torque measurements $F$. Force is available during both training and deployment and may update independently of visual--language inference. We start from a pretrained force-conditioned VLA teacher $T_\theta$, which is frozen during residual distillation. The demonstrations used to train this teacher contain synchronized observations and executed actions, but no labels that separate each action into a slow reference and a force-responsive correction.

Actions are expressed in the end-effector frame as
\begin{equation}
    A = [\, p \in \mathbb{R}^{3};\; r \in \mathbb{R}^{6};\; g \in \mathbb{R} \,] \in \mathbb{R}^{10},
    \label{eq:action_space}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the continuous 6D rotation representation~\cite{zhou2019continuity}, $g$ is the gripper command, and $A^{p}=[p;r]\in\mathbb{R}^{9}$ denotes the pose component. A continuous rotation representation is required because the targets defined below are action differences, which an Euler parameterization would corrupt at its branch cut.

Let $t_k$ denote the observation timestamp of the most recent slow update and $t$ the current control instant. The command is a reference plus a correction,
\begin{equation}
    A^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)
    =
    A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)
    +
    \Delta A(t),
    \qquad
    \Delta A(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{9},
    \label{eq:action_comp}
\end{equation}
where $A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ is interpolated from the action chunk generated at $t_k$ and the correction acts on pose alone; the gripper channel remains with the slow branch, since grasp transitions are semantic rather than reactive.

This factorization reduces the computation required for reactive updates: the reference evolves at the slower semantic timescale and is obtained by interpolation, whereas only $\Delta A$ is recomputed when new force measurements arrive. Demonstrations, however, supervise only the complete action and do not identify its reference and residual components. Any pair $(A^{\mathrm{ref}}, \Delta A)$ that sums to the demonstrated action is consistent with behavior cloning, so the reference may absorb contact-dependent behavior while the residual redundantly reproduces nominal motion. We therefore use the frozen teacher to construct an explicit target for $\Delta A$.

\subsection{Counterfactual Residual Target Construction}
\label{sec:teacher}

\subsubsection{Force-conditioned teacher.}

The teacher extends ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla}, a $\pi_0$-style flow-matching policy~\cite{black2024pi0}, by replacing its instantaneous force representation with a causal temporal encoder. For each teacher inference timestamp $t_k$ we retrieve a fixed-duration window
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_k
    =
    \left\{
        F(\tau)
        \mid
        t_k-w \leq \tau \leq t_k
    \right\}
    \label{eq:force_window}
\end{equation}
from the timestamped native-rate stream rather than downsampling it to the visual observation rate. A validity mask distinguishes a partially populated window from one reporting genuine zero force. A causal temporal convolutional network encodes the window,
\begin{equation}
    z_k^F
    =
    E_T^F(\mathcal{F}_k),
    \label{eq:teacher_tcn}
\end{equation}
projected to the force-fusion dimension of the base model. A single force token is retained so that the downstream fusion and action-generation pathway is unchanged, giving the factual prediction
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}
    =
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_k^F\right)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 10}.
    \label{eq:full_teacher}
\end{equation}
The window spans contact onset, impact transients, and recovery, which a single force sample at the visual rate cannot represent.

\subsubsection{Adapted null-force mode.}
\label{sec:null_decomp}

To query the same teacher without supplying the current measurement, we introduce a learnable token
\begin{equation}
    z_{\varnothing F} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_F}
\end{equation}
at the position where $z_k^F$ enters the fusion module. Being shared across samples, it denotes the absence of an instance-specific force observation rather than a physical zero, and can encode a dataset-level prior but not the current force history.

Substituting this token alone leaves the policy degraded, which would make the difference defined below reflect degradation rather than the force condition. We therefore allow the null branch to recover. With all teacher weights frozen, $z_{\varnothing F}$ is optimized jointly with a rank-$\rho$ adapter that corrects the pose channels of the flow velocity in null mode only,
\begin{equation}
    v^{p} \leftarrow v^{p} + W_{\mathrm{out}}\,\sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{in}}\,h\right),
    \quad
    W_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\rho \times d},\;
    W_{\mathrm{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times \rho},
    \label{eq:nominal_adapter}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the action-expert hidden state and $\sigma$ the SiLU nonlinearity, under the teacher's action regression loss against the demonstrated action,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\varnothing}
    =
    d\!\left(
        T_{\theta}(V_k,L,S_k,z_{\varnothing F}),\;
        A_k^{\mathrm{expert}}
    \right).
    \label{eq:null_training}
\end{equation}
Both $z_{\varnothing F}$ and $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ are zero-initialized, so optimization starts from the unadapted null path. The factual branch bypasses the adapter entirely and is therefore unchanged. Eq.~\eqref{eq:null_training} yields an adapted null-force \emph{mode} of the same teacher rather than a separately trained reference policy, and this mode is retained as the slow branch at deployment.

\subsubsection{Paired target extraction.}
\label{sec:teacher_differencing}

Because the teacher is a flow-matching sampler, two independent queries would differ by both the force condition and the sampling noise. We therefore compute the vision--language prefix and its key/value cache once and initialize both integrations from a single noise draw $\epsilon$,
\begin{align}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}
    &=
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_k^F;\,\epsilon\right),
    \label{eq:teacher_full_again}
    \\
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}
    &=
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_{\varnothing F};\,\epsilon\right),
    \quad
    \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
    \label{eq:teacher_null}
\end{align}
Coupling $\epsilon$ removes sampling variation from the comparison; Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_ablation} evaluates the effect of independent draws. Sharing the prefix also avoids a redundant vision--language pass during offline extraction.

The null-conditioned chunk serves as the reference,
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    =
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}},
    \label{eq:nominal_target}
\end{equation}
and the factual--counterfactual difference is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{cf}}
    =
    \left(A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}\right)^{p}
    -
    \left(A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}\right)^{p}
    \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 9}.
    \label{eq:teacher_residual}
\end{equation}
We call $\Delta A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{cf}}$ the \emph{counterfactual residual}: the teacher's output change between the factual measured-force condition and its adapted null-force counterfactual. The term is used at the model level rather than in a causal-identification sense. Because the null-specific adapter is active only in the counterfactual branch, and because vision and state may retain contact cues, the residual represents the complete conditional mode change rather than a uniquely attributable force effect.

\subsection{High-Rate Residual Policy}
\label{sec:residual_policy}

\subsubsection{Slow reference and cached intent.}
\label{sec:slow}

The slow branch is the frozen teacher in null mode. Each slow update yields the reference chunk $A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}$ and, from the same forward pass, a compact context for the high-rate branch. The vision--language prefix $H_k \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times d}$ contains several hundred tokens and is compressed by mean pooling into $M$ contiguous bins under the prefix mask,
\begin{equation}
    Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}
    =
    \mathrm{Pool}_M\!\left(H_k, m_k\right)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d},
    \label{eq:intent_projection}
\end{equation}
with an accompanying validity mask. Pooling is fixed rather than learned, so the deployment-time context is reproducible from the live prefix without additional parameters; a learned projector inside the student maps these bins to its own width.

Between slow updates the reference is interpolated at the teacher's action period $\tau_A$ from the cached chunk, linearly for pose and held for the gripper. The high-rate branch receives no fresh image, and visual information reaches it only through $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}$, whose staleness is reported to the student by the time feature of Sec.~\ref{sec:rate_agnostic}. This follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher_residual}, in which the shared vision--language prefix is held fixed when the target is defined.

\subsubsection{Residual student.}
\label{sec:fast}

The student consumes force and emits pose corrections. A causal TCN of the same family as the teacher's encodes the native-rate window,
\begin{equation}
    Z_t^F
    =
    E_S^F\!\left(\mathcal{F}_t\right),
    \label{eq:student_force}
\end{equation}
with the validity mask applied at the encoder input so that unpopulated slots do not contribute.

The remaining inputs form an unordered set of conditions: the cached intent bins, the current state, the current reference action, and a time token. Each is projected to a common width and tagged with a learned type embedding,
\begin{equation}
    X_t
    =
    \left[
        \widetilde{Z}_k^{\mathrm{intent}};\;
        Z_t^F;\;
        P_S(S_t);\;
        P_A(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t));\;
        P_\Gamma(\gamma_t);\;
        q
    \right],
    \label{eq:fast_token_sequence}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_t$ is the time feature of Sec.~\ref{sec:rate_agnostic} and $q$ a learned residual query. Positional encodings are omitted, since the conditions admit no meaningful ordering. A single pre-norm block with grouped-query attention processes the set, with the conditions attending to one another and the query reading them without being visible in return,
\begin{equation}
    H_t = \mathrm{Block}(X_t),
    \label{eq:gemma}
\end{equation}
and a linear head maps the query state to the emitted chunk,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \hat{A}_{t,1:K}
    =
    W_{\mathrm{res}}\,H_t[q]
    \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times 9}.
    \label{eq:residual_output}
\end{equation}
With $W_{\mathrm{res}}$ zero-initialized, the untrained student predicts no correction and the composed policy begins from $A^{\mathrm{cmd}} = A^{\mathrm{ref}}$.

Emitting $K$ steps spaced by $\tau_A$, rather than a single correction, tolerates the student's own latency and an unknown deployment rate: when an update is late or the actuator outruns the student, later steps of the previous chunk remain valid commands. The labels stay causal even though they extend forward in time, because the teacher generated its entire chunk from the single observation at $t_k$; no future force or state enters $\Delta A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{cf}}$.

\subsubsection{Training objective.}
\label{sec:loss}

Only the student is trained. Let $\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,1:K}$ and $A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,1:K}$ denote the leading $K$ steps of the teacher targets and $w_j \propto \beta^{\,j-1}$ normalized step weights. The first term supervises the correction itself,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_j \,
    d\!\left(
        \Delta \hat{A}_{t,j},\;
        \Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,j}
    \right),
    \label{eq:res_loss}
\end{equation}
and the second constrains the composed command to reproduce the factual teacher,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_j \,
    d\!\left(
        \left(A^{\mathrm{ref}}_j\right)^{p} + \Delta \hat{A}_{t,j},\;
        \left(A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,j}\right)^{p}
    \right),
    \label{eq:comp_loss}
\end{equation}
giving
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =
    \lambda_{\mathrm{res}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{comp}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}.
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
The decay $\beta < 1$ weights the first step, which executes in the common case, above later steps, which execute only when the student stalls or is outrun. No reference-imitation term is required because the reference is supplied by the frozen teacher, and no behavior-cloning term is applied to the student because it would mix total-action imitation with the counterfactual-residual target.

\subsection{Timing-Randomized Training and Execution}
\label{sec:rate_agnostic}

A student trained at one slow rate and latency learns that operating point. We instead treat timing as a distribution. Targets are extracted once at the full observation rate, and each training epoch redraws a schedule: a slow update period from $[1/f_{\max}, 1/f_{\min}]$, an inference latency $\ell$ from $[\ell_{\min}, \ell_{\max}]$, and per-update jitter from $[-\eta,\eta]$. A packet published at $t_k$ becomes usable only at $t_k+\ell$, so control instants before that are excluded and a packet overtaken in flight by a newer one is discarded. The student consequently never observes a zero-age context, which is not realizable on hardware.

The student is conditioned on the state of its context through
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_t
    =
    \left[
        \frac{t - t_k}{c},\;
        \alpha_t
    \right],
    \qquad
    \alpha_t = \frac{t - t_j}{\tau_A} \in [0,1),
    \label{eq:time_features}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is a fixed age scale covering the randomized band and $t_j$ the chunk waypoint preceding $t$. The first feature accumulates across a slow period and the second is periodic within it, so together they identify the staleness of the semantic context and the position of the command inside an action interval. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} summarizes the resulting procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Counterfactual residual distillation}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE demonstrations $\mathcal{D}$, teacher $T_\theta$, chunk length $K$
\STATE freeze $\theta$; optimize $z_{\varnothing F}$, $(W_{\mathrm{in}},W_{\mathrm{out}})$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:null_training}
\FOR{each observation $k$ in $\mathcal{D}$}
\STATE compute prefix $H_k$ and its key/value cache; draw $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$
\STATE $A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,k}\!\leftarrow\!T_\theta(\cdot,z_k^F;\epsilon)$, \quad $A^{\mathrm{null}}_{T,k}\!\leftarrow\!T_\theta(\cdot,z_{\varnothing F};\epsilon)$
\STATE $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{Pool}_M(H_k,m_k)$; \; $\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,k}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher_residual}
\STATE store $(A^{\mathrm{null}}_{T,k},\,\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,k},\,Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}},\,t_k)$
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each training epoch}
\STATE redraw slow period, latency $\ell$, and jitter; assign packets to observations
\STATE form $A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ by interpolation and $\gamma_t$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:time_features}
\STATE minimize Eq.~\eqref{eq:total_loss} over the student parameters
\ENDFOR
\ENSURE high-rate residual student
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Computational cost.}
\label{sec:complexity}

Cached-prefix slow--fast policies retain an action expert in the high-rate loop, so each update costs $N_{\mathrm{flow}}$ denoising passes over that expert, or $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{flow}} L_{\mathrm{AE}} d_{\mathrm{AE}}^{2})$ for depth $L_{\mathrm{AE}}$ and width $d_{\mathrm{AE}}$. Our reactive update is one deterministic pass over a small network, or $\mathcal{O}(L_{S} d_{S}^{2})$, reducing both the step count and the depth--width product.

\subsubsection{Asynchronous execution.}
\label{sec:execution}

At deployment the teacher runs in null mode on its own thread and publishes a packet containing the reference chunk, the pooled intent bins, the observation timestamp, and the time at which the packet became available. Force is appended to a ring buffer at the sensor rate and state is updated independently.

The student occupies a second thread. Each cycle it reads the most recent packet, force window, and state, and publishes a command chunk stamped with its validity interval. A third loop samples that chunk at the control instant and applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_comp}, rebasing the reference onto the state observed by its own packet rather than the current state. Separating publication from consumption keeps the actuator from blocking on inference: a late student update degrades to a later step of the previous chunk, the case for which Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_output} is supervised. When no unexpired chunk is available, the actuator holds instead of extrapolating.
