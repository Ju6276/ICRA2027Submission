\subsection{Problem Formulation and Overview}
\label{sec:problem}

We study contact-rich manipulation in which a policy observes images $V$, a language instruction $L$, robot state $S$, and a timestamped stream of force/torque measurements $F$. Force is available during both training and deployment and may update independently of visual--language inference. We start from a pretrained force-conditioned VLA teacher $T_\theta$, which is frozen during residual distillation. The demonstrations used to train this teacher contain synchronized observations and executed actions, but no labels that separate each action into a slow reference and a force-responsive correction.

Actions are expressed in the end-effector frame as
\begin{equation}
    A = [\, p \in \mathbb{R}^{3};\; r \in \mathbb{R}^{6};\; g \in \mathbb{R} \,] \in \mathbb{R}^{10},
    \label{eq:action_space}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the continuous 6D rotation representation~\cite{zhou2019continuity}, $g$ is the gripper command, and $A^{p}=[p;r]\in\mathbb{R}^{9}$ denotes the pose component.

Let $t_k$ denote the observation timestamp of the most recent slow update and $t$ the current control instant. The command is a reference plus a correction,
\begin{equation}
    A^{\mathrm{cmd}}(t)
    =
    A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)
    +
    \Delta A(t),
    \qquad
    \Delta A(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{9},
    \label{eq:action_comp}
\end{equation}
where $A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ is interpolated from the action chunk generated at $t_k$ and the correction acts on pose alone; the gripper channel remains with the slow branch, since grasp transitions are semantic rather than reactive.

This factorization reduces the computation required for reactive updates: the reference evolves at the slower semantic timescale and is obtained by interpolation, whereas only $\Delta A$ is recomputed when new force measurements arrive. Demonstrations, however, supervise only the complete action and do not identify its reference and residual components. Any pair $(A^{\mathrm{ref}}, \Delta A)$ that sums to the demonstrated action is consistent with behavior cloning. Consequently, the reference may absorb contact-dependent behavior while the residual redundantly reproduces nominal motion. We therefore use the pretrained and frozen teacher $T_\theta$ to construct an explicit target for $\Delta A$.

\subsection{Counterfactual Residual Target Construction}
\label{sec:teacher}

\subsubsection{Force-conditioned teacher.}

The teacher extends ForceVLA~\cite{yu2025forcevla}, a $\pi_0$-style flow-matching policy~\cite{black2024pi0}, by replacing its instantaneous force representation with a causal temporal encoder. For each teacher inference timestamp $t_k$ we retrieve a fixed-duration window
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_k
    =
    \left\{
        F(\tau)
        \mid
        t_k-w \leq \tau \leq t_k
    \right\},
    \label{eq:force_window}
\end{equation}
drawn from the timestamped native-rate stream rather than downsampled to the visual observation rate. Samples carry a validity mask, distinguishing a partially populated window from one reporting genuine zero force. A causal temporal convolutional network encodes the window,
\begin{equation}
    z_k^F
    =
    E_T^F(\mathcal{F}_k),
    \label{eq:teacher_tcn}
\end{equation}
projected to the force-fusion dimension of the base model. A single force token is retained so that the downstream multimodal fusion and action-generation pathway is unchanged, giving
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}
    =
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_k^F\right)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 10}.
    \label{eq:full_teacher}
\end{equation}
The temporal encoder exposes short-horizon interaction dynamics such as contact onset, impact transients, oscillation, and recovery, which a single force sample at the visual rate cannot represent.

\subsubsection{Learning the null-force reference.}
\label{sec:null_decomp}

To query the teacher without supplying the current measurement we introduce a learnable token
\begin{equation}
    z_{\varnothing F} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_F},
\end{equation}
inserted where $z_k^F$ enters the fusion module. It denotes the absence of an instance-specific force observation rather than a physical zero, and being shared across samples it can encode a dataset-level force prior but not the current force history.

Withholding force degrades the policy, and a degraded branch would render the difference of Sec.~\ref{sec:teacher_differencing} a measure of degradation rather than of force. The null branch is therefore allowed to recover. With all teacher weights frozen, $z_{\varnothing F}$ is optimized jointly with a rank-$\rho$ adapter that corrects the pose channels of the flow velocity in null mode only,
\begin{equation}
    v^{p} \leftarrow v^{p} + W_{\mathrm{out}}\,\sigma\!\left(W_{\mathrm{in}}\,h\right),
    \quad
    W_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\rho \times d},\;
    W_{\mathrm{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times \rho},
    \label{eq:nominal_adapter}
\end{equation}
with $h$ the action-expert hidden state and $\sigma$ the SiLU nonlinearity, under the teacher's action regression loss against the demonstrated action,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\varnothing}
    =
    d\!\left(
        T_{\theta}(V_k,L,S_k,z_{\varnothing F}),\;
        A_k^{\mathrm{expert}}
    \right).
    \label{eq:null_training}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:null_training} yields an adapted null-force \emph{mode} of the same teacher rather than a separately trained reference policy. This mode is retained as the slow branch at deployment.

\subsubsection{Paired target extraction.}
\label{sec:teacher_differencing}

The teacher is frozen and queried twice. Since it is a flow-matching sampler, two independent queries would differ by both the force condition and the sampling noise. We therefore share every remaining source of variation: the vision--language prefix and its key/value cache are computed once, and a single noise draw $\epsilon$ initializes both integrations,
\begin{align}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}
    &=
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_k^F;\,\epsilon\right),
    \label{eq:teacher_full_again}
    \\
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}
    &=
    T_{\theta}\!\left(V_k,L,S_k,z_{\varnothing F};\,\epsilon\right),
    \quad
    \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
    \label{eq:teacher_null}
\end{align}
Coupling $\epsilon$ removes sampling variation from the comparison; Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_ablation} evaluates the effect of using independent draws. Sharing the prefix also avoids a redundant vision--language pass during offline extraction.

The null-conditioned chunk serves as the reference,
\begin{equation}
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    =
    A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}},
    \label{eq:nominal_target}
\end{equation}
and their factual--counterfactual difference is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{cf}}
    =
    \left(A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{full}}\right)^{p}
    -
    \left(A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}\right)^{p}
    \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 9}.
    \label{eq:teacher_residual}
\end{equation}
We call $\Delta A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{cf}}$ the \emph{counterfactual residual}: it is the teacher's output change between the factual measured-force condition and its adapted null-force counterfactual. The term counterfactual is used at the model level rather than in a causal-identification sense. Because the null-specific adapter is active only in the counterfactual branch, and because vision and state may retain contact cues, the residual represents the complete conditional mode change rather than a uniquely attributable force effect.

\subsection{High-Rate Residual Policy}
\label{sec:residual_policy}

\subsubsection{Slow reference and cached intent.}
\label{sec:slow}

The slow branch is the frozen teacher in null mode; no nominal policy is distilled. Each slow update yields the reference chunk $A_{T,k}^{\mathrm{null}}$ and, from the same forward pass, a compact context for the high-rate branch. The vision--language prefix $H_k \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times d}$ contains several hundred tokens and is compressed by mean pooling into $M$ contiguous bins under the prefix mask,
\begin{equation}
    Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}
    =
    \mathrm{Pool}_M\!\left(H_k, m_k\right)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d},
    \label{eq:intent_projection}
\end{equation}
with an accompanying validity mask. Pooling is fixed rather than learned so that the deployment-time context is reproducible from the live prefix without additional parameters; a learned attention projector within the student maps these bins to its own width. The intent tokens are not constrained to reproduce any teacher hidden state and serve only as task context for the correction.

Between slow updates the reference is interpolated at the teacher's action period $\tau_A$ from the cached chunk, and the staleness of that chunk is exposed to the student (Sec.~\ref{sec:rate_agnostic}). The high-rate branch receives no fresh image; visual information reaches it only through $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}$. This design follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher_residual}, in which the shared vision--language prefix is held fixed when defining the correction target.

\subsubsection{Residual student.}
\label{sec:fast}

The student consumes force and emits pose corrections. A causal TCN of the same family as the teacher's encodes a shorter window of the newest native-rate measurements,
\begin{equation}
    Z_t^F
    =
    E_S^F\!\left(F_{t-w_S:t}\right),
    \label{eq:student_force}
\end{equation}
with the validity mask applied at the encoder input so that unpopulated slots do not contribute.

The remaining inputs form an unordered set of conditions: the cached intent bins, the current state, the current reference action, and a time token. Each is projected to a common width and tagged with a learned type embedding,
\begin{equation}
    X_t
    =
    \left[
        \widetilde{Z}_k^{\mathrm{intent}};\;
        Z_t^F;\;
        P_S(S_t);\;
        P_A(A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t));\;
        P_\Gamma(\gamma_t);\;
        q
    \right],
    \label{eq:fast_token_sequence}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_t$ is the time feature of Sec.~\ref{sec:rate_agnostic} and $q$ a learned residual query. Positional encodings are omitted, the conditions admitting no meaningful ordering. A single pre-norm block with grouped-query attention processes the set, conditions attending to one another bidirectionally while the query is excluded from their view,
\begin{equation}
    H_t = \mathrm{Block}(X_t),
    \label{eq:gemma}
\end{equation}
and a linear head maps the query state to the emitted chunk,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \hat{A}_{t,1:K}
    =
    W_{\mathrm{res}}\,H_t[q]
    \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times 9},
    \label{eq:residual_output}
\end{equation}
with $W_{\mathrm{res}}$ zero-initialized. The initial student therefore predicts zero correction, so the composed policy begins from $A^{\mathrm{cmd}} = A^{\mathrm{ref}}$.

Emitting $K$ steps spaced by $\tau_A$ rather than a single correction provides tolerance to the student's own latency and to an unknown deployment rate: when an update is late or the actuator outruns the student, later steps of the previous chunk remain valid commands. The construction is causal despite extending forward in time, because the teacher generated its entire chunk from the single observation at $t_k$; step $j$ is the teacher's response to the currently observable force $j$ action periods ahead, and no future force or state enters the label.

\subsubsection{Training objective.}
\label{sec:loss}

Only the student is trained. Let $\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,1:K}$ and $A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,1:K}$ denote the leading $K$ steps of the teacher targets and $w_j \propto \beta^{\,j-1}$ normalized step weights. The first term preserves residual specialization,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_j \,
    d\!\left(
        \Delta \hat{A}_{t,j},\;
        \Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,j}
    \right),
    \label{eq:res_loss}
\end{equation}
and the second constrains the composed command to reproduce the full-force teacher,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_j \,
    d\!\left(
        \left(A^{\mathrm{ref}}_j\right)^{p} + \Delta \hat{A}_{t,j},\;
        \left(A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,j}\right)^{p}
    \right),
    \label{eq:comp_loss}
\end{equation}
giving
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =
    \lambda_{\mathrm{res}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}
    +
    \lambda_{\mathrm{comp}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}.
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
The decay $\beta < 1$ reflects that the first step executes in the common case while later steps execute only when the student stalls or is outrun. No reference-imitation term appears because the slow reference is supplied by the frozen teacher, and no behavior-cloning term is applied to the student because it would mix total-action imitation with the counterfactual-residual target.

\subsection{Timing-Randomized Training and Execution}
\label{sec:rate_agnostic}

A student trained at a single slow rate and latency learns that operating point. We instead treat timing as a distribution. Targets are extracted once at full rate, and each training epoch redraws a schedule: a slow update period from $[1/f_{\max}, 1/f_{\min}]$, an inference latency $\ell$ from $[\ell_{\min}, \ell_{\max}]$, and per-update jitter from $[-\eta,\eta]$. A packet published at $t_k$ becomes usable only at $t_k + \ell$; earlier control instants are excluded, and a packet overtaken in flight by a newer one is discarded. Modeling latency explicitly also excludes the physically unrealizable case of a zero-age context, which unconstrained extraction would otherwise admit.

The student is then conditioned on the value of its context,
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_t
    =
    \left[
        \frac{t - t_k}{c},\;
        \alpha_t
    \right],
    \qquad
    \alpha_t = \frac{t - t_j}{\tau_A} \in [0,1),
    \label{eq:time_features}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is a fixed age scale covering the randomized band and $\alpha_t$ the interpolation fraction between the chunk waypoints bracketing $t$. The two features are not collinear: age accumulates across a slow period while $\alpha_t$ is periodic within it, so jointly they identify both the staleness of the semantic context and the position of the command within an action interval.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} summarizes the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Target generation and student training}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE demonstrations $\mathcal{D}$, teacher $T_\theta$, chunk length $K$
\STATE \emph{Null-mode adaptation:} freeze $\theta$, then optimize $z_{\varnothing F}$ and $(W_{\mathrm{in}},W_{\mathrm{out}})$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:null_training}
\STATE \emph{Paired target extraction:}
\FOR{each observation row $k$ in $\mathcal{D}$}
\STATE compute prefix $H_k$ and its key/value cache; draw $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$
\STATE $A^{\mathrm{full}}_{T,k}\!\leftarrow\!T_\theta(\cdot,z_k^F;\epsilon)$, \quad $A^{\mathrm{null}}_{T,k}\!\leftarrow\!T_\theta(\cdot,z_{\varnothing F};\epsilon)$
\STATE $Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{Pool}_M(H_k,m_k)$, \quad $\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,k}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:teacher_residual}
\STATE store $(A^{\mathrm{null}}_{T,k},\,\Delta A^{\mathrm{cf}}_{T,k},\,Z_k^{\mathrm{intent}},\,t_k)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \emph{Student training:}
\FOR{each epoch}
\STATE redraw slow period, latency $\ell$, and jitter; assign packets to rows
\STATE form $A^{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ by interpolation and $\gamma_t$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:time_features}
\STATE minimize Eq.~\eqref{eq:total_loss} over the student parameters
\ENDFOR
\ENSURE high-rate residual student
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Computational cost.}
\label{sec:complexity}

Existing fast--slow force policies cache the vision--language prefix but retain an action expert in the high-rate loop, so each update costs $N_{\mathrm{flow}}$ denoising passes over that expert. Ours costs one deterministic pass over a small network. Writing $L_{\mathrm{AE}},d_{\mathrm{AE}}$ and $L_{S},d_{S}$ for the respective depths and widths, the per-update cost is $\mathcal{O}\!\left(N_{\mathrm{flow}} L_{\mathrm{AE}} d_{\mathrm{AE}}^{2}\right)$ against $\mathcal{O}\!\left(L_{S} d_{S}^{2}\right)$, with both the step count and the depth--width product reduced.

\subsubsection{Asynchronous execution.}
\label{sec:execution}

At deployment the teacher runs in null mode as the slow branch on its own thread, publishing a packet containing the reference chunk, the pooled intent bins, the observation timestamp, and the time at which the packet became available. Force is appended to a ring buffer at the sensor rate and state is updated independently.

The student occupies a second thread. Each cycle it reads the most recent packet, force window, and state, and publishes a command chunk stamped with its validity interval. A third loop samples that chunk at the control instant and applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_comp}, rebasing the reference onto the state observed by its own packet rather than the current state. Separating publication from consumption prevents the actuator from blocking on inference, since a late student update degrades to executing a later step of the previous chunk, the case for which Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_output} is supervised. When no unexpired chunk is available the actuator holds rather than extrapolating.
