\subsection{Force-Aware Manipulation Policies}
Force-aware VLAs incorporate force/torque through multimodal fusion, prompting, temporal memory, hybrid force--position control, or force-aware optimization~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace,li2026fmvlaforcebasedmemoryvisionlanguageaction,zhang2026craftadaptingvlamodels}, while force-centric imitation jointly models motion and interaction forces from demonstrations~\cite{liu2025forcemimicforcecentricimitationlearning}. These approaches retain task motion and physical adaptation within a unified policy output. FD-VLA instead distills a latent force representation from vision and state for sensor-free inference~\cite{zhao2026fdvlaforcedistilledvisionlanguageactionmodel}; ForceDelta-VLA retains measured wrench feedback and distills the teacher's force-conditioned action deviation.

\subsection{Multi-Rate and Structured Manipulation Policies}
Visuomotor methods learn from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams, adapt diffusion inference frequency across interaction phases, or introduce fast correction and hybrid force--position pathways~\cite{lee2025manipforceforceguidedpolicylearning,chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion,wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy,fang2026forcepolicylearninghybrid,zhuo2026fardp}. VLA methods also separate modality-update rates. FAVLA runs a slow semantic backbone and a variable-rate force-conditioned action expert that regenerates action chunks~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla}; DAM-VLA refreshes modality-specific latent buffers at their sensor rates and reads them with a $100$-Hz action head~\cite{vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. Real-Time Chunking (RTC) instead addresses inference latency in flow-based chunk policies by asynchronously generating the next chunk and inpainting its uncommitted actions for continuity with the executing chunk~\cite{black2025realtimechunking}. ForceDelta-VLA addresses a complementary problem: it retains the generative VLA output as a cached reference and constructs offline supervision for an explicit, force-conditioned additive correction together with a reference-staleness correction, without regenerating a full action chunk at each fast update.

\subsection{Residual Policy Learning}
Residual policy learning augments a nominal controller with a learned correction and has been applied to contact-rich manipulation~\cite{johannink2018residualreinforcementlearningrobot}. Existing methods obtain residual supervision through reinforcement learning or on-policy human corrections, including tactile-conditioned adaptation of frozen visual policies and reactive force experts trained with DAgger~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving,yu2026omnitactunepolicyagnosticrealworldrl,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla}. Other imitation approaches prescribe an additive correction structure directly~\cite{wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy}. ForceDelta-VLA instead derives its target from paired force-conditioned and missing-force teacher predictions.

\subsection{Policy Distillation}
Learned missing-modality representations distinguish an unavailable condition from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024exploringmissingmodalitymultimodal,nezakati2024mmprobustmultimodallearning}, motivating a distinct missing-force operating point. Robot-policy distillation commonly compresses iterative diffusion or flow sampling into a fast full-action generator~\cite{wang2024onestepdiffusionpolicyfast,dong2026flowsteprealtimemultimodal}, while guidance distillation transfers conditional updates through paired predictions~\cite{ho2022classifierfreediffusionguidance,meng2023distillationguideddiffusionmodels}. ForceDelta-VLA retains the generative output as the task-level reference and distills an action-space force deviation plus a reference-state correction, rather than a complete action generator.
