\subsection{Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models}
Force and tactile feedback reveal physical interaction that is ambiguous or invisible in images. ForceVLA treats six-axis force/torque sensing as a first-class VLA modality and fuses it with visual--language features through a force-aware mixture of experts~\cite{yu2025forcevla}. ForceVLA2 adds stage-level force prompts, a direct raw-force pathway, and hybrid force--position control~\cite{li2026forcevla2}. TaF-VLA aligns tactile and force representations, while Tactile-VLA and VLA-Touch connect physical feedback to high-level reasoning and downstream control~\cite{huang2026tafvla,huang2025tactilevla,bi2025vlatouch}. These works primarily study how force or touch should be represented, aligned, and fused into a VLA. FoRe-VLA instead assumes that force is available during both training and deployment and asks how its contribution to corrective behavior can be explicitly supervised.

\subsection{Reactive and Asynchronous Physical Feedback}
High-frequency physical sensing is easily lost when all modalities are synchronized to the visual or VLA inference rate. ManipForce preserves asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams through frequency-aware representations~\cite{lee2025manipforce}. Reactive Diffusion Policy uses a slow visual planner and a fast tactile feedback controller~\cite{xue2025rdp}. FAVLA, DAM-VLA, AT-VLA, and T-Rex similarly introduce fast force/tactile processing, native-rate modality updates, or variable-rate experts for responsive manipulation~\cite{li2026favla,vanjani2026damvla,li2026atvla,niu2026trex}. PhaForce combines low-rate planning with phase-scheduled high-rate residual correction~\cite{wang2026phaforce}. These approaches motivate our asynchronous deployment, but their primary contribution is architectural routing or execution scheduling. Our central problem is complementary: given a nominal branch and a force-reactive branch, we derive separate behavioral targets rather than relying on the architecture alone to induce specialization.

\subsection{Null-Modality Modeling and Behavioral Distillation}
Learned missing-modality tokens replace absent inputs with shared trainable embeddings, avoiding the semantic ambiguity between an unavailable measurement and a physically valid zero value~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}. Masked Modality Projection instead predicts missing-modality tokens from the modalities that remain available~\cite{nezakati2024mmp}. In robotics, FD-VLA distills a force representation from vision and robot state so that force-aware inference can proceed without a physical force sensor~\cite{zhao2026fdvla}. These methods target robustness to missing modalities or removal of sensing requirements.

FoRe-VLA adopts the learned-token mechanism for a different purpose. A shared null-force token supplies no instance-specific force measurement to the frozen teacher and therefore defines a non-force-conditioned reference query. We subtract this output from the measured-force output to construct a policy-space residual and distill the two outputs into specialized students. We do not reconstruct missing force, use a contrastive loss, or remove the force sensor at deployment. The resulting residual represents the teacher's output change under full versus null force conditioning, not a guaranteed causal attribution to force.
