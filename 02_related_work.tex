\subsection{Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action Policies}
Force/torque feedback reveals contact onset, load, and constrained motion that can remain ambiguous in images. ForceVLA introduces six-axis force/torque measurements through a force-aware mixture-of-experts fusion module~\cite{yu2025forcevla}. ForceVLA2 combines stage-level force prompts with a direct force pathway and hybrid force--position control~\cite{li2026forcevla2}, while TA-VLA examines where temporal torque features should enter a pretrained VLA decoder~\cite{zhang2025tavla}. These designs differ in fusion depth and control interface, but all condition the generation of complete action chunks on physical feedback.

This formulation improves contact awareness without separating the slowly varying task plan from the correction induced by newly observed force. Consequently, a force update remains coupled to the action-generation pathway, and the demonstrations provide no explicit label for the corrective component of the action.

\subsection{Slow--Fast Force-Responsive Control}
Slow--fast policies reorganize computation around the different timescales of semantic perception and physical interaction. FAVLA caches slow visual--language context and repeatedly evaluates a force-conditioned action expert, with its update frequency scheduled from predicted force variation~\cite{li2026favla}. LIFT similarly reuses a visual--language prefix but injects recent force memory into a reactive action stream trained with online corrections~\cite{lv2026lift}. DAM-VLA instead maintains modality-specific latent buffers that are refreshed at their native rates and read asynchronously by the action expert~\cite{vanjani2026damvla}.

Related non-VLA policies address the same rate mismatch at the visuomotor level. ManipForce learns frequency-aware representations from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams~\cite{lee2025manipforce}, whereas ImplicitRDP imposes a causal slow--fast structure within a visual--force diffusion policy~\cite{chen2025implicitrdp}. These methods preserve high-rate physical feedback, but their reactive pathways remain complete-action predictors; rate separation alone does not define which part of an action should be assigned to fast correction.

\subsection{Residual Learning and Conditional Distillation}
Residual policies preserve a base controller and learn an additive correction, but require a signal that identifies the correction separately from the executed action. ResiP obtains this signal from task reward, training a closed-loop residual policy with reinforcement learning on top of a frozen action-chunking imitation policy~\cite{ankile2025resip}. Compliant Residual DAgger instead collects on-policy kinesthetic delta corrections and trains a force-conditioned residual from those additional demonstrations~\cite{xu2025crdagger}. Both approaches define a residual through information beyond the original offline behavior-cloning labels.

Conditional modeling offers a complementary mechanism for constructing such supervision. Learned missing-modality tokens distinguish an unavailable observation from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}. In generative models, paired conditional and unconditional predictions isolate the effect of a condition for classifier-free guidance, and guidance distillation transfers that paired computation into a cheaper predictor~\cite{ho2022cfg,meng2023guidancedistill}. These ideas provide the ingredients for deriving an action-space correction from a pretrained conditional policy, without reward optimization or newly collected corrective demonstrations.
