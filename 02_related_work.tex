\subsection{Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models}
Force and tactile feedback reveal physical interaction that is ambiguous or invisible in images. ForceVLA treats six-axis force/torque sensing as a first-class VLA modality and fuses it with visual--language features through a force-aware mixture of experts~\cite{yu2025forcevla}. ForceVLA2 adds stage-level force prompts, a direct raw-force pathway, and hybrid force--position control~\cite{li2026forcevla2}. TA-VLA studies where torque should enter a pretrained VLA, finding that a torque adapter belongs in the decoder, that torque history is most effective when summarized into a single token, and that predicting torque as an auxiliary output improves performance~\cite{zhang2025tavla}. TaF-VLA aligns tactile and force representations, while Tactile-VLA and VLA-Touch connect physical feedback to high-level reasoning and downstream control~\cite{huang2026tafvla,huang2025tactilevla,bi2025vlatouch}. FD-VLA pursues the opposite direction, distilling a force representation from vision and state so that inference requires no physical sensor~\cite{zhao2026fdvla}. This line of work studies how force should be represented, aligned, and fused. We assume force is available during both training and deployment, and take such a model as the teacher to be decomposed.

\subsection{Reactive and Asynchronous Physical Feedback}
High-frequency physical sensing is easily lost when all modalities are synchronized to the visual or VLA inference rate. ManipForce preserves asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams through frequency-aware representations~\cite{lee2025manipforce}, and Reactive Diffusion Policy pairs a slow visual planner with a fast tactile controller~\cite{xue2025rdp}. FAVLA couples a slow vision--language backbone that emits a key/value context with a fast action expert conditioned on the latest force sequence, scheduling the expert's execution count from a predicted force-variance signal~\cite{li2026favla}. LIFT grafts a reactive action expert onto a pretrained policy, injects a causal force memory through zero-initialized cross attention, caches the vision--language prefix, and aligns the force window to a fixed inference-latency offset~\cite{lv2026lift}. Fast-in-Slow embeds a high-rate system inside a slow reasoning backbone through parameter sharing~\cite{fisvla2025}, while DAM-VLA, AT-VLA, T-Rex, and PhaForce introduce native-rate modality updates, variable-rate experts, or phase-scheduled high-rate correction~\cite{vanjani2026damvla,li2026atvla,niu2026trex,wang2026phaforce}. Compliant Residual DAgger learns a fast residual over a slow base policy from on-policy rollouts with human corrections~\cite{xu2025crdagger}.

Two properties distinguish our setting. In the above systems the high-rate branch is an action generator, so caching removes the perception cost while the generation cost continues to bound the attainable rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:complexity}); we assign that branch a strictly smaller target. Their timing is also fixed at training time, whether as a rate ratio~\cite{li2026favla}, a latency offset~\cite{lv2026lift}, or a uniform resampling of all modalities to a common rate, whereas we treat it as a distribution and expose the measured context age to the student. Our supervision differs from Compliant Residual DAgger in origin: its residual targets come from on-policy interaction with human corrections, ours from an offline counterfactual query on a frozen teacher.

\subsection{Null-Conditioned Queries and Counterfactual Differencing}
Learned missing-modality tokens replace absent inputs with shared trainable embeddings, avoiding the ambiguity between an unavailable measurement and a physically valid zero~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}, and Masked Modality Projection predicts missing-modality tokens from the modalities that remain~\cite{nezakati2024mmp}. We adopt the learned-token mechanism not for robustness to a missing sensor, but because it provides a query that withholds the current measurement while keeping the teacher in distribution.

Differencing a generative model under a removed condition is established practice. Classifier-free guidance forms the conditional-minus-unconditional difference at inference time to amplify conditioning~\cite{ho2022cfg}, and guidance distillation trains a student to reproduce the guided combination in a single pass, halving per-step cost~\cite{meng2023guidancedistill}. Both operate on a same-size model at a single rate and are agnostic to modality; we use the difference to supervise a separate and smaller module executing at a different frequency.

Counterfactual modality intervention has also been applied to embodied policies. Counterfactual Modality Attribution quantifies modality-level contributions in multimodal language models through Shapley values over coupled counterfactuals~\cite{cma2026modality}, and Embodied Interpretability scores the causal influence of VLA inputs by divergence between full-information and counterfactual policies~\cite{embodiedinterp2026}. CofactVLA constructs a language-masked counterfactual branch to remove a visual shortcut~\cite{cofactvla2026}. Closest in mechanism, IDR applies zero-padding modality interventions to a frozen VLA, measures the norm of the induced output change, and fuses it back as a gated residual at test time~\cite{idr2026causal}. These methods explain, debias, or adjust a single model at a single rate, and none trains a separate module on the induced difference. A further distinction concerns the intervention itself: a zero-padded force input lies outside the teacher's training distribution, so the induced change conflates the force contribution with degradation under an invalid input, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:null_training} yields a competent force-free mode. Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_decomposition} compares the two interventions empirically.

Following the caution of prior attribution work~\cite{cma2026modality}, we do not claim the deviation is a causal force effect. Vision and state condition both branches, so the deviation is the teacher's output change under full versus null force conditioning.
