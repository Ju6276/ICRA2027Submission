\subsection{Force-Aware and Multi-Rate Manipulation Policies}
Force-aware manipulation policies incorporate interaction feedback to improve contact-rich control. Recent VLA-based approaches integrate force/torque through multimodal fusion, force prompting, temporal force memory, or force-aware optimization~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace,li2026fmvlaforcebasedmemoryvisionlanguageaction,zhao2026fdvlaforcedistilledvisionlanguageactionmodel,zhang2026craftadaptingvlamodels}. Beyond VLAs, force-centric imitation methods jointly model motion and interaction forces from demonstrations, as exemplified by ForceMimic~\cite{liu2025forcemimicforcecentricimitationlearning}. These methods improve contact-sensitive action generation, but typically predict task motion and physical adjustment within a unified policy output.

Multi-rate and structured control policies instead separate task-level motion from faster physical adaptation. Visuomotor approaches learn from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams or combine low-rate planning with a structurally separated fast correction pathway~\cite{lee2025manipforceforceguidedpolicylearning,chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion,wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy}. Force Policy explicitly decomposes contact-rich manipulation into global motion and local hybrid force-position control under an interaction frame~\cite{fang2026forcepolicylearninghybrid}. Among VLAs, FAVLA reuses cached semantic features for faster force-conditioned action inference, LIFT post-trains a force-memory-conditioned reactive expert with corrective rollouts, and DAM-VLA refreshes modality-specific latent buffers at their sensor rates~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla,vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. These approaches separate update rates or control roles through fast pathways, hybrid force-position control, caching, or asynchronous latent updates. Our focus is complementary: rather than prescribing the corrective component architecturally or obtaining it from additional interaction data, we derive explicit additive-correction supervision from an offline force-aware teacher and execute the distilled correction independently of full-action generation.

\subsection{Residual Policy Learning}
Residual policy learning combines a nominal controller with a learned correction and has long been used to refine robot behavior, including contact-rich manipulation~\cite{johannink2018residualreinforcementlearningrobot}. Classical residual reinforcement learning identifies corrective behavior through task rewards, while more recent residual refinement methods improve pretrained manipulation policies through reinforcement learning or additional on-policy human corrections~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving}. Other fast-correction approaches impose an additive residual structure directly during imitation~\cite{wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy}. In all of these cases, the residual is defined through rewards, corrective interaction, or an explicitly prescribed decomposition. Our setting provides none of these supervisory signals: we construct the residual target offline from matched full-force and null-force predictions of a pretrained force-aware teacher.

\subsection{Policy Distillation}
Learned missing-modality representations distinguish an unavailable condition from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024exploringmissingmodalitymultimodal,nezakati2024mmprobustmultimodallearning}, motivating a distinct null operating point rather than zeroing the force input. Recent robot-policy distillation compresses iterative diffusion or flow policies into single-step action generators for faster closed-loop control~\cite{wang2024onestepdiffusionpolicyfast,dong2026flowsteprealtimemultimodal}, while guidance distillation transfers model-dependent conditional updates using paired predictions~\cite{ho2022classifierfreediffusionguidance,meng2023distillationguideddiffusionmodels}. Unlike full-policy distillation, we retain the generative VLA as the reference and distill only its corrective action-space deviations.
