\subsection{Force-Aware Manipulation Policies}
Force-aware VLAs incorporate force/torque through multimodal fusion, prompting, temporal memory, or force-aware optimization~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace,li2026fmvlaforcebasedmemoryvisionlanguageaction,zhao2026fdvlaforcedistilledvisionlanguageactionmodel,zhang2026craftadaptingvlamodels}, while force-centric imitation jointly models motion and interaction forces from demonstrations~\cite{liu2025forcemimicforcecentricimitationlearning}. These approaches improve contact-sensitive prediction but generally retain task motion and physical adaptation within a unified policy output.

Visuomotor methods learn from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams, adapt diffusion inference frequency across interaction phases, or introduce fast correction and hybrid force--position pathways~\cite{lee2025manipforceforceguidedpolicylearning,chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion,wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy,fang2026forcepolicylearninghybrid,zhuo2026fardp}. VLA methods similarly reuse cached semantic features, train reactive experts with corrective rollouts, or refresh modality-specific latent states at their sensor rates~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla,vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. These designs change the schedule or architecture through which physical feedback affects control. ForceDelta-VLA instead derives explicit additive-correction supervision from matched full-force and null-force predictions of an offline force-aware teacher and executes the distilled correction independently of full-action generation.

\subsection{Residual Policy Learning}
Residual policy learning augments a nominal controller with a learned correction and has been applied to contact-rich manipulation~\cite{johannink2018residualreinforcementlearningrobot}. Existing methods obtain residual supervision through reinforcement learning or on-policy human corrections, including tactile-conditioned residual adaptation of frozen visual policies~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving,yu2026omnitactunepolicyagnosticrealworldrl}. Other imitation approaches prescribe an additive correction structure directly~\cite{wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy}. Across these lines, residuals are defined through rewards, corrective interaction, or a prescribed decomposition. ForceDelta-VLA instead constructs explicit residual targets offline from matched full-force and learned null-force predictions of a pretrained force-aware teacher.

\subsection{Policy Distillation}
Learned missing-modality representations distinguish an unavailable condition from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024exploringmissingmodalitymultimodal,nezakati2024mmprobustmultimodallearning}, motivating a distinct null operating point rather than zeroing the force input. Recent robot-policy distillation compresses iterative diffusion or flow policies into single-step action generators for faster closed-loop control~\cite{wang2024onestepdiffusionpolicyfast,dong2026flowsteprealtimemultimodal}, while guidance distillation transfers model-dependent conditional updates using paired predictions~\cite{ho2022classifierfreediffusionguidance,meng2023distillationguideddiffusionmodels}. Unlike full-policy distillation, we retain the generative VLA as the reference and distill only its corrective action-space deviations.
