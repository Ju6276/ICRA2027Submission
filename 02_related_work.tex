\subsection{Force-Aware and Temporal Manipulation}
Force and tactile feedback provide direct observations of physical interaction that are often difficult to infer from vision alone. Recent VLA approaches have therefore incorporated force as an explicit modality. ForceVLA introduces a force-aware mixture-of-experts architecture to fuse six-axis force/torque measurements with visual-language representations for contact-rich manipulation, while ForceVLA2 further develops explicit force-aware reasoning and hybrid force-position control. Beyond instantaneous force fusion, ManipForce focuses on the temporal mismatch between RGB and high-frequency F/T sensing, using frequency-aware representations and cross-modal fusion to preserve asynchronous physical information. arXiv These works demonstrate the importance of force-aware and temporally rich physical representations. In our framework, temporal force encoding is used to construct a stronger unified teacher rather than being the primary source of novelty.

\subsection{Slow-Fast and Residual Contact Policies}
A growing body of work separates semantic reasoning from high-frequency physical reaction. FAVLA decouples a slow VLM from a faster force-conditioned action expert, allowing reactive action generation from recent force sequences while avoiding repeated execution of the full VLM. Related asynchronous policies similarly exploit the different temporal characteristics of semantic and physical modalities. Residual formulations further combine nominal motion with fast corrective control. PhaForce, for example, coordinates low-rate chunk-level planning with high-rate residual correction using explicit contact- and phase-dependent scheduling. These studies establish that slow-fast execution and force-conditioned residual correction are effective for contact-rich manipulation. Our focus is different: rather than introducing another temporal decomposition, we study how the behavioral responsibility of each branch is determined. Existing methods largely define this responsibility through architecture, phase schedules, or direct supervision of the reactive branch. We instead derive nominal and corrective targets from the behavior of a unified force-aware teacher.

\subsection{Physical-Modality Distillation}
Knowledge distillation has also been explored for transferring physical sensing capabilities into VLA policies. FD-VLA uses real force observations during training to learn a distilled force representation that can subsequently be inferred from vision and robot state, removing the need for a physical force sensor at deployment. Related tactile-distillation approaches similarly transfer physical-modality knowledge into policies with reduced sensing requirements.

Our objective is complementary but distinct. We retain high-frequency force sensing during deployment and do not attempt to reconstruct force representations from other modalities. Instead, we distill a behavioral contrast from a force-aware teacher: the difference between its behavior with online force information and under a force-unavailable condition. This contrast supervises a specialized fast residual policy, while the force-unavailable behavior provides supervision for the slow nominal policy. Thus, our distillation targets the division of behavioral responsibility across temporal scales, rather than the physical modality itself.
