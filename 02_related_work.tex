\subsection{Force-Aware Manipulation Policies}
General-purpose VLAs such as $\pi_{0.5}$ predict actions from vision, language, and robot state without measured force/torque feedback~\cite{intelligence2025pi05visionlanguageactionmodelopenworld}. Force-aware VLAs incorporate force/torque through multimodal fusion, prompting, temporal memory, hybrid force--position control, or force-aware optimization~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace,li2026fmvlaforcebasedmemoryvisionlanguageaction,zhang2026craftadaptingvlamodels}, while force-centric imitation jointly models motion and interaction forces from demonstrations~\cite{liu2025forcemimicforcecentricimitationlearning}. These methods typically predict task motion and contact adjustment together. FD-VLA instead distills a latent force representation from vision and state for inference without measured wrench feedback~\cite{zhao2026fdvlaforcedistilledvisionlanguageactionmodel}; ForceDelta-VLA uses measured wrench feedback to update action corrections.

\subsection{Multi-Rate and Structured Manipulation Policies}
Visuomotor methods learn from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams, adapt diffusion inference frequency across interaction phases, or introduce fast correction and hybrid force--position pathways~\cite{lee2025manipforceforceguidedpolicylearning,chen2026implicitrdpendtoendvisualforcediffusion,wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy,fang2026forcepolicylearninghybrid,zhuo2026fardp}. VLA methods also separate modality-update rates. FAVLA runs a slow semantic backbone and a variable-rate force-conditioned action expert that regenerates action chunks~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla}; DAM-VLA refreshes modality-specific latent buffers at their sensor rates and reads them with a $100$-Hz action head~\cite{vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. RTC preserves continuity between asynchronously generated action chunks through inpainting~\cite{black2025realtimechunking}, whereas ForceDelta-VLA reuses reference actions and updates corrections independently of their generation.

\subsection{Residual Policy Learning}
Correction policy learning adds a learned correction to a base controller and has been applied to contact-rich manipulation~\cite{johannink2018residualreinforcementlearningrobot}. Residual reinforcement learning and corrective imitation obtain supervision through rewards or on-policy human corrections, with applications including tactile-conditioned adaptation of frozen visual policies and reactive force experts trained with DAgger~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving,yu2026omnitactunepolicyagnosticrealworldrl,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla}. Other imitation approaches directly define an additive correction structure~\cite{wang2026phaforcephasescheduledvisualforcepolicy}. ForceDelta-VLA instead derives its target from paired force-conditioned and force-agnostic teacher predictions.

\subsection{Policy Distillation}
Robot-policy distillation commonly compresses iterative diffusion or flow sampling into a fast full-action generator~\cite{wang2024onestepdiffusionpolicyfast,dong2026flowsteprealtimemultimodal}, while guidance distillation transfers conditional updates through paired predictions~\cite{ho2022classifierfreediffusionguidance,meng2023distillationguideddiffusionmodels}. ForceDelta-VLA distills corrections while retaining the generative policy for reference actions. Its learned force-agnostic mode follows work on missing-modality representations that distinguish an unavailable condition from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024exploringmissingmodalitymultimodal,nezakati2024mmprobustmultimodallearning}.
