\subsection{Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action Policies}
Force-aware VLAs incorporate physical feedback through three main routes. The first directly conditions action generation on measured force/torque, using mechanisms such as mixture-of-experts fusion, force prompts with a direct force pathway, or temporal torque injection at selected decoder layers~\cite{yu2025forcevla,li2026forcevla2,zhang2025tavla}. The second learns a force-related representation from other observations, allowing force-aware inference without a physical force sensor~\cite{zhao2026fdvla}. The third modifies optimization so that force is not dominated by higher-dimensional visual and language features; curriculum training with an information bottleneck is one such approach~\cite{zhang2026craft}. These lines of work establish complementary strategies for learning contact-sensitive complete-action policies. They do not, however, expose a separately supervised correction that can be updated independently of the full action generator.

\subsection{Fast--Slow and Asynchronous Force Control}
Multi-rate force control has been studied at both the visuomotor and VLA levels. Visuomotor policies preserve physical feedback either by learning directly from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams or by imposing a causal fast--slow structure on diffusion-based action generation~\cite{lee2025manipforce,chen2025implicitrdp}. VLA systems instead reuse slowly changing semantic computation while refreshing force-dependent representations or actions more frequently. FAVLA repeatedly evaluates a force-conditioned action expert from cached semantic features and adapts its rate to predicted force variation~\cite{li2026favla}. LIFT adds a force-memory-conditioned reactive action expert and obtains the contact distribution needed for post-training through online corrective rollouts~\cite{lv2026lift}. DAM-VLA updates modality-specific latent buffers at their sensor rates and continuously reads them through a shared action head~\cite{vanjani2026damvla}. Thus, these methods differ in whether the physical update is produced by a fast action expert, an online-trained reactive stream, or asynchronously refreshed latent state. They separate computation or modality update rates, whereas the problem considered here is how to obtain an explicitly supervised additive correction from the original offline demonstrations.

\subsection{Residual Learning and Conditional Distillation}
Residual learning separates nominal behavior from online correction, but requires supervision that identifies the correction rather than only the executed action. Existing approaches obtain this signal through reinforcement learning on task rewards or through additional on-policy corrective demonstrations~\cite{ankile2025resip,xu2025crdagger}. Both sources can train effective residual controllers, but require information beyond the original offline imitation dataset.

Conditional modeling offers an alternative basis for constructing supervision from a pretrained model. Missing-modality representations distinguish an unavailable input from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}; paired conditional and unconditional predictions isolate a condition-dependent update, which can subsequently be transferred to a single predictor through distillation~\cite{ho2022cfg,meng2023guidancedistill}. Together, these ideas enable a model-defined correction target to be formed from paired predictions, without treating their difference as the unique causal contribution of the removed modality.
