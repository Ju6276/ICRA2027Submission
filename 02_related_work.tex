\subsection{Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models}
Force and tactile feedback reveal physical interaction that is ambiguous or invisible in images. ForceVLA treats six-axis force/torque sensing as a first-class VLA modality and fuses it with visual--language features through a force-aware mixture of experts~\cite{yu2025forcevla}. ForceVLA2 adds stage-level force prompts, a direct raw-force pathway, and hybrid force--position control~\cite{li2026forcevla2}. TaF-VLA aligns tactile and force representations, while Tactile-VLA and VLA-Touch connect physical feedback to high-level reasoning and downstream control~\cite{huang2026tafvla,huang2025tactilevla,bi2025vlatouch}. FD-VLA takes the opposite direction and distills a force representation from vision and state so that inference needs no physical sensor~\cite{zhao2026fdvla}. These works primarily study how force or touch should be represented, aligned, and fused. We assume force is available during both training and deployment, and take such a model as the teacher to be decomposed.

\subsection{Reactive and Asynchronous Physical Feedback}
High-frequency physical sensing is easily lost when all modalities are synchronized to the visual or VLA inference rate. ManipForce preserves asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams through frequency-aware representations~\cite{lee2025manipforce}, and Reactive Diffusion Policy pairs a slow visual planner with a fast tactile controller~\cite{xue2025rdp}. FAVLA runs a slow vision--language backbone that emits a key/value context and a fast action expert that consumes the latest force sequence, additionally scheduling the expert's execution count from a predicted force-variance signal~\cite{li2026favla}. LIFT grafts a reactive action expert onto a pretrained policy, injects a causal force memory through zero-initialized cross attention, caches the vision-language prefix, and aligns the force window to a fixed inference-latency offset~\cite{lv2026lift}. Fast-in-Slow embeds a high-rate system inside a slow reasoning backbone by sharing parameters~\cite{fisvla2025}; DAM-VLA, AT-VLA, T-Rex, and PhaForce introduce native-rate modality updates, variable-rate experts, or phase-scheduled high-rate correction~\cite{vanjani2026damvla,li2026atvla,niu2026trex,wang2026phaforce}. Compliant Residual DAgger instead learns a fast residual on top of a slow base policy from on-policy rollouts with human corrections~\cite{xu2025crdagger}.

We adopt this deployment structure and depart from it in two respects. First, in all of the above the high-rate branch remains an action generator: caching removes the vision-language forward pass, but each update still pays a full action-expert denoise, which is what pins these systems near $13$--$33$~Hz (Sec.~\ref{sec:complexity}). We instead give the high-rate branch a strictly smaller task. Second, their timing is fixed at training time --- as a rate ratio~\cite{li2026favla}, a latency offset~\cite{lv2026lift}, or a uniform resampling of all modalities to one rate --- whereas we randomize it and expose the measured context age to the student. We also differ from Compliant Residual DAgger in supervision: its residual targets come from on-policy interaction and human corrections, ours from an offline counterfactual query on a frozen teacher. That work reports on-policy correction to be important, which bounds what a purely offline construction such as ours can be expected to achieve; we return to this in Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\subsection{Null-Conditioned Queries and Counterfactual Differencing}
Learned missing-modality tokens replace absent inputs with shared trainable embeddings, avoiding the semantic ambiguity between an unavailable measurement and a physically valid zero value~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}; Masked Modality Projection instead predicts missing-modality tokens from the modalities that remain~\cite{nezakati2024mmp}. We use the learned-token mechanism for its second property rather than its first: we are not robust to a missing sensor, we need a query that withholds the current measurement while keeping the teacher in distribution.

Differencing a generative model under a removed condition is itself established. Classifier-free guidance forms the conditional-minus-unconditional difference at inference time to amplify conditioning~\cite{ho2022cfg}, and guidance distillation trains a student to reproduce the guided \emph{combination} in one pass, halving per-step cost~\cite{meng2023guidancedistill}. The distinction from our work is what the difference is used for: guidance distillation compresses a same-size model at the same rate and does not concern modalities, whereas we use the difference as the learning target of a separate, much smaller module running at a different frequency.

Counterfactual modality intervention has also reached embodied policies. Counterfactual Modality Attribution quantifies modality-level contributions in multimodal LLMs via Shapley values over coupled counterfactuals~\cite{cma2026modality}, and Embodied Interpretability scores the causal influence of VLA inputs by divergence between full-information and counterfactual policies~\cite{embodiedinterp2026}. CofactVLA builds a language-masked counterfactual branch to remove a visual shortcut~\cite{cofactvla2026}. Closest in mechanism, IDR performs zero-padding modality interventions on a frozen VLA, measures the norm of the induced output change, and fuses it back as a gated residual at test time~\cite{idr2026causal}.

Our construction differs from these on both counts. They use the counterfactual difference to explain, debias, or adjust one model at one rate, and none trains a separate module on it; we use it to factorize a policy across time scales, which is what yields the order-of-magnitude change in the high-rate path. And where IDR intervenes by zero-padding, we adapt a learned null-force condition on the frozen teacher: a zero-padded force input is off the teacher's training distribution, so the induced output change conflates the force contribution with the teacher's degradation under an invalid input. We evaluate this difference directly in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_decomposition}. Consistent with prior caution~\cite{cma2026modality}, we do not claim the deviation is a causal force effect: vision and state remain conditioning inputs to both branches, so the deviation is the teacher's output change under full versus null force conditioning, and nothing stronger.
