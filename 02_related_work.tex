\subsection{Force-Aware and Multi-Rate VLA Control}
Force-aware VLAs incorporate measured force/torque through multimodal fusion, force prompting, or temporal feature injection, while related approaches distill force representations from other observations or rebalance force learning during optimization~\cite{yu2025forcevla,li2026forcevla2,zhang2025tavla,zhao2026fdvla,zhang2026craft}. Conventional force-conditioned VLA policies improve contact-sensitive action generation but typically encode nominal motion and physical adjustment within a complete action prediction.

Multi-rate policies separate the update schedules of semantic and physical computation. Visuomotor approaches learn from asynchronous RGB and force/torque streams or impose a causal fast--slow diffusion structure~\cite{lee2025manipforce,chen2025implicitrdp}. Among VLAs, FAVLA reuses cached semantic features for faster force-conditioned action inference, LIFT trains a force-memory-conditioned reactive expert using online corrective rollouts, and DAM-VLA refreshes modality-specific latent buffers at their sensor rates~\cite{li2026favla,lv2026lift,vanjani2026damvla}. These systems differ in execution architecture, but their physical pathway remains an action predictor, a separately trained reactive stream, or an asynchronously updated latent representation. They primarily separate when physical information is updated. We instead identify what an additive correction should predict from offline teacher supervision and execute that correction independently of full-action generation.

\subsection{Residual Policy Learning}
Residual policies combine a nominal controller with an online correction, making them well suited to physical interaction. The correction, however, must be identified by supervision beyond the executed action. Existing methods obtain this signal from reinforcement learning with task rewards or from additional on-policy corrective demonstrations~\cite{ankile2025resip,xu2025crdagger}. Our setting provides neither source: we derive the correction offline from a pretrained force-aware teacher and the demonstrations used to train it.

\subsection{Conditional Distillation}
Learned missing-modality representations distinguish an unavailable condition from a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}. Conditional and unconditional prediction differences can also define model-dependent updates that are transferred to a single predictor through guidance distillation~\cite{ho2022cfg,meng2023guidancedistill}. We adapt these ideas to construct a teacher-defined action correction from paired full-force and null-force predictions, without interpreting their difference as a unique causal attribution to force.
