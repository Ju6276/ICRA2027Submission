\subsection{Force-Aware Robot Policies}
Force and tactile sensing expose interaction states that are ambiguous in vision. ForceVLA and ForceVLA2 integrate force into VLA action generation through multimodal fusion, force prompts, and direct force pathways~\cite{yu2025forcevla,li2026forcevla2}; TA-VLA and TaF-VLA study temporal torque encoding and tactile--force representation alignment~\cite{zhang2025tavla,huang2026tafvla}. Other systems combine tactile reasoning with hybrid or downstream control~\cite{huang2025tactilevla,bi2025vlatouch}. These methods establish the value of physical feedback but do not isolate a compact correction target for a separately executable high-rate branch. ForceRes-VLA assumes force is available during training and deployment and uses a force-aware VLA as its teacher.

\subsection{Slow--Fast and Asynchronous Control}
Rate separation is a common response to the disparity between semantic reasoning and physical interaction. Reactive Diffusion Policy combines slow visual planning with fast tactile control~\cite{xue2025rdp}; FAVLA and LIFT reuse cached visual--language context in reactive action experts~\cite{li2026favla,lv2026lift}. ManipForce, DAM-VLA, AT-VLA, T-Rex, PhaForce, and related systems preserve native-rate feedback or coordinate experts across rates~\cite{lee2025manipforce,vanjani2026damvla,li2026atvla,niu2026trex,wang2026phaforce,fisvla2025}. Residual policies instead correct a slower base controller, sometimes using on-policy human feedback~\cite{xu2025crdagger}. ForceRes-VLA shares the slow--fast execution principle, but its fast module predicts only a teacher-defined residual rather than regenerating a complete action chunk. It is trained offline and explicitly models variations in slow-policy age and latency.

\subsection{Missing-Modality Conditioning and Paired Differencing}
Learned missing-modality tokens provide an explicit representation of unavailable input and avoid conflating absence with a physically meaningful zero~\cite{ramazanova2024mmt}; masked-modality projection instead predicts missing features from available modalities~\cite{nezakati2024mmp}. We use a learned null token together with a small null-specific adapter to create a competent alternate mode of a frozen teacher, not to make the deployed policy robust to sensor failure.

Conditional differencing is also used in classifier-free guidance and its distillation to measure or amplify the effect of conditioning~\cite{ho2022cfg,meng2023guidancedistill}. In embodied models, modality interventions have been used for interpretation, shortcut mitigation, and inference-time reweighting~\cite{cma2026modality,embodiedinterp2026,cofactvla2026,idr2026causal}. Our objective differs: paired full/null queries construct an output-space training target for a smaller module operating at a different rate. Because null mode contains adapted parameters and the remaining observations may encode contact, we describe the target as a conditional policy deviation, not a causal attribution or a contrastive-learning objective.
