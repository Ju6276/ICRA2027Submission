Force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies improve contact-rich manipulation by conditioning action generation on interaction feedback~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace}. Yet they typically represent task-level motion and contact-dependent adjustment within the same generative action prediction. At deployment, task-level behavior can be reused across nearby control cycles, whereas physical correction should adapt to newly observed contact. This coupling leaves the correction without an explicit supervision target or an independently executable output (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}).

An additive residual can retain a missing-force reference while exposing a pose-space correction, but offline demonstrations record only the executed action. Multiple reference--residual splits can reconstruct the same label, and directly subtracting a cached reference further mixes force-conditioned deviation with reference staleness. Moreover, a measured zero wrench is a valid contact condition, distinct from withholding the wrench history. These ambiguities motivate constructing residual supervision from matched queries of a frozen teacher.

Existing asynchronous policies separate computation or sensor-update rates~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla,vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. Residual reinforcement learning and corrective imitation obtain the correction signal from rewards or extra on-policy interaction~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving}. We instead construct the supervision offline and keep asynchronous execution.

ForceDelta-VLA constructs both learned targets from the cached context available to the fast pathway. Matched force-conditioned and missing-force teacher queries share a visual--language prefix and flow noise; their action difference supervises the force residual, while a second target captures the remaining discrepancy between the cached reference and the cached-context missing-force prediction. The known query-to-current state displacement is rebased deterministically, and the learned corrections execute independently of generative inference.

We evaluate ForceDelta-VLA on five single-arm and four bimanual real-robot tasks spanning contact establishment, precision insertion, friction-dominated motion, and constrained manipulation. The proposed method achieves an $82.2\%$ mean success rate, compared with $54.4\%$ for ForceVLA and $72.8\%$ for ImplicitRDP, while reducing peak contact force by approximately $16\%$ and $19\%$ on the single-arm and bimanual platforms, respectively.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce \textbf{missing-force residual distillation}: an explicit teacher-defined force-responsive residual target from matched force-conditioned and learned missing-force predictions of a frozen teacher, using only the original offline dataset;
    \item We formulate a \textbf{reference-state-consistent dual-residual decomposition} that separately supervises force-conditioned correction and cached-reference staleness while rebasing the known state displacement deterministically; and
    \item We develop an \textbf{independently executable residual pathway} that updates pose corrections from recent force history while reusing cached task context and missing-force references.
\end{enumerate}
