Force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies improve contact-rich manipulation by conditioning action generation on interaction feedback~\cite{yu2025forcevlaenhancingvlamodels,li2026forcevla2unleashinghybridforceposition,zhang2025tavlaelucidatingdesignspace}. Yet they typically represent task-level motion and contact-dependent adjustment within the same generative action prediction. At deployment, task-level behavior can be reused across nearby control cycles, whereas physical correction should adapt to newly observed contact. This coupling leaves the correction without an explicit supervision target or an independently executable output (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}).

An additive force-responsive residual provides an explicit pose-space correction while retaining the task-level behavior of a force-agnostic reference. Offline demonstrations do not identify this factorization because they provide executed actions but no labels for the reference or residual. Direct action supervision can therefore assign contact-dependent behavior to the reference or task-level motion to the residual.

Subtracting a cached reference from the demonstrated action mixes physical correction with reference staleness. A zero-force query represents a valid physical state rather than missing force conditioning. These ambiguities motivate constructing an explicit correction target from a pretrained force-aware policy and its existing offline data.

Existing asynchronous policies separate computation or sensor-update rates~\cite{li2026favlaforceadaptivefastslowvla,wang2026lateforceacceleratingvla,vanjani2026damvladecoupledasynchronousmultimodal}. Residual reinforcement learning and corrective imitation instead obtain correction signals from task rewards or additional on-policy interaction~\cite{ankile2024imitationrefinementresidual,xu2025compliantresidualdaggerimproving}. We study offline target construction for a correction that executes independently of generative action inference.

ForceDelta-VLA learns a null-mode reference, constructs separate force and staleness targets from frozen-teacher predictions, and distills them into a residual policy that executes independently of generative action inference.

We evaluate ForceDelta-VLA on five single-arm and four bimanual real-robot tasks spanning contact establishment, precision insertion, friction-dominated motion, and constrained manipulation. The proposed method achieves an $82.2\%$ mean success rate, compared with $54.4\%$ for ForceVLA and $72.8\%$ for ImplicitRDP, while reducing peak contact force by approximately $19\%$ relative to ImplicitRDP on both platforms.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce \textbf{null-mode residual distillation}, which constructs an explicit force-responsive correction target from matched full-force and learned null-force predictions of a frozen force-aware teacher, without task rewards or additional corrective or on-policy demonstrations;
    \item We formulate a \textbf{reference-state-consistent dual-residual decomposition} that separately supervises force-conditioned correction and cached-reference staleness, preventing temporal reference mismatch from being absorbed into the force residual; and
    \item We develop an \textbf{independently executable residual pathway} that updates pose corrections from current force feedback while reusing cached task context and null-mode references.
\end{enumerate}
