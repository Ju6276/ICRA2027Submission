\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide strong semantic priors for general-purpose manipulation, but contact-rich behavior requires direct and timely force feedback. Existing force-aware VLAs determine where force enters the network, and recent reactive architectures determine how frequently it is processed. Neither design choice, however, explicitly identifies which part of a demonstrated action should be assigned to force-conditioned correction. When a nominal branch and a force-reactive branch are trained only against the same final action, their behavioral decomposition is underdetermined. We introduce \emph{FoRe-VLA}, a teacher-guided framework for distilling force-conditioned action residuals. A temporal force-aware teacher is first trained with synchronized vision, language, robot state, and native-rate force/torque history. After freezing the teacher, we query it twice for the same observation: once with the measured force history and once with a learned null-force token. The null-conditioned output provides a reference that is not conditioned on the current force measurement, while output-space differencing yields a force-conditioned residual target. These targets supervise a slow vision--language--state policy that predicts nominal action chunks and semantic intent, and a lightweight fast policy that predicts residual corrections from live high-frequency force feedback. This procedure is teacher output differencing rather than contrastive learning, and the residual is not assumed to be a causal force contribution. At deployment, the teacher and null token are removed; both force sensing and the fast force-conditioned student remain active, enabling responsive correction during contact-rich manipulation.
\end{abstract}
