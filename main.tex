\begin{abstract}
Force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies improve contact-rich manipulation, but typically entangle task-level motion and contact-dependent adjustment within a single generative action prediction. Offline demonstrations provide no labels for decomposing that prediction into a reusable reference and an explicit correction. We present ForceDelta-VLA, a residual-distillation framework that constructs a force-responsive target from paired full-force and learned null-force predictions, without task rewards or additional corrective or on-policy demonstrations. A separate residual compensates for reference staleness under replayed deployment schedules. The resulting residual policy reuses cached task context and timestamped null-mode references while updating pose corrections from recent force history, without regenerating complete actions. Across nine single-arm and bimanual contact-rich tasks, ForceDelta-VLA achieves an $82.2\%$ mean success rate, compared with $54.4\%$ for ForceVLA and $72.8\%$ for the fast--slow visual--force baseline ImplicitRDP. It also reduces peak contact force by approximately $19\%$ relative to ImplicitRDP on both platforms.
\end{abstract}
