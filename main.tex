\begin{abstract}
Contact-rich manipulation requires correction within a control cycle, but the fast branch of current slow--fast force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still regenerates a complete action chunk. Even with the vision-language prefix fully cached, that branch pays a full flow-matching denoise per update: we measure $76$~ms on an RTX~4090, or $13$~Hz, an order of magnitude below a $100$~Hz force sensor. We argue the high-rate branch does not need to reproduce the action at all --- only the part of it attributable to force, which is a far simpler function. Demonstrations, however, label the total action and never this component. We obtain the label by querying a frozen temporal force-aware teacher twice on the same observation: once with the measured force history and once with a learned null-force condition, under a shared vision-language prefix and shared flow-matching noise so that the force condition is the only difference between the two samples. The resulting deviation supervises a lightweight residual student that consumes live force at the sensor rate, and the teacher's null-conditioned chunk serves directly as the slow reference, so no separate nominal policy is distilled. The student needs $44.7$~M parameters and one deterministic forward pass, measured at $2.4$~ms ($417$~Hz), a $31.8\times$ reduction in the high-rate path. Because the teacher's update period, inference latency, and jitter are randomized when the targets are generated, and the student is conditioned on the measured age of its context, a single trained student is deployable across slow-branch rates it never saw. We report the latency analysis and the offline decomposition diagnostics; task-level claims await the robot evaluation described in Sec.~\ref{sec:experiment}.
\end{abstract}
