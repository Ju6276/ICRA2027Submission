\begin{abstract}
Contact-rich manipulation requires physical corrections on a shorter timescale than semantic task planning. Existing force-conditioned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies couple force feedback with complete action generation, preventing such corrections from being updated independently of generative inference. We present ForceDelta-VLA, a framework that distills a pretrained force-aware VLA into an asynchronously executable residual policy. Paired predictions under full-force and learned null-force modes provide explicit supervision for force-conditioned correction without task rewards or additional corrective demonstrations. A complementary target teaches the residual policy to compensate for a reference that becomes stale during asynchronous inference. At deployment, the VLA produces timestamped reference actions, while a compact policy uses recent force history and cached task context to update pose corrections. Across eight single-arm and bimanual contact-rich tasks, ForceDelta-VLA improves mean success rate by \assumed{12.5 percentage points}, reduces peak contact force by \assumed{18.7\%}, and shortens completion time by \assumed{14.3\%} relative to the strongest baseline.
\end{abstract}
