\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide strong semantic priors for general-purpose manipulation, but contact-rich behavior remains difficult because vision alone does not fully reveal rapidly changing physical interactions. Force-aware VLAs address this limitation by incorporating force/torque observations, yet their inference rate is typically much lower than the rate at which contact dynamics evolve. Slow--fast policies can react more frequently, but supervision of only the final action leaves the functional division between nominal motion and force-dependent correction ambiguous. We introduce \emph{ForceSplit}, a teacher-guided nominal--residual distillation framework that learns this division from a unified temporal force-aware VLA teacher. The frozen teacher is queried with its observed force history and with a learned null-force token. Its null-conditioned prediction defines a nominal target, while the behavioral difference between full-force and null-force predictions defines a force-conditioned residual target. These complementary targets supervise a slow vision--language--state policy that generates nominal action chunks and semantic intent, and a lightweight fast policy that continuously predicts residual corrections from native-rate force history, robot state, and the cached slow-policy context. Nominal, residual, composition, and behavior-cloning objectives jointly preserve the teacher's force-aware behavior while explicitly assigning responsibilities across temporal scales. At deployment, the teacher is removed and the two students execute asynchronously, enabling semantic reasoning at the VLA rate and high-frequency physical correction in the robot control loop.
\end{abstract}
