\begin{abstract}
Contact-rich manipulation requires corrective action within a control cycle, yet the high-rate branch of current slow--fast force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still regenerates a complete action chunk. Caching the vision--language prefix removes the perception cost but not the generation cost, so each high-rate update performs a full flow-matching denoise and the branch remains an order of magnitude slower than a contemporary force sensor. We argue that this branch need not reproduce the action, only the component of it attributable to force. Demonstrations, however, label the total action and never this component, leaving the decomposition underdetermined. We recover the missing label by querying a frozen temporal force-aware teacher twice on the same observation, once with the measured force history and once with a learned null-force condition, under a shared vision--language prefix and a shared flow-matching noise draw so that the force condition is the only difference between the two samples. The deviation between the two outputs supervises a lightweight residual student that consumes force at the sensor rate, while the teacher's null-conditioned chunk serves directly as the slow reference, so that no separate nominal policy is distilled. Replacing action generation in this branch with a single deterministic forward pass reduces its measured per-update cost by more than an order of magnitude, placing it within the sampling period of a $100$~Hz force sensor. We additionally randomize the slow update period, inference latency, and jitter while generating targets and condition the student on the measured age of its cached context, and examine whether one trained student transfers across slow-branch rates unseen in training. We evaluate on eight contact-rich single-arm and bimanual manipulation tasks against $\pi_{0.5}$, ForceVLA, and TA-VLA.

\end{abstract}
