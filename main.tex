\begin{abstract}
Force-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies improve contact-rich manipulation, but typically entangle nominal task motion and contact-dependent adjustment within a single generative action prediction. Offline demonstrations provide no labels for decomposing that prediction into a reusable reference and an explicit correction. We present ForceDelta-VLA, a residual-distillation framework that constructs a teacher-defined correction target from paired full-force and learned null-force predictions, without task rewards or additional corrective or on-policy demonstrations. A complementary target compensates for references that become stale during delayed inference. The resulting independently executable residual pathway reuses cached task context and timestamped null-mode references while updating pose corrections from recent force history, without regenerating complete actions. Across eight single-arm and bimanual contact-rich tasks, ForceDelta-VLA improves mean success rate by \assumed{$X$ percentage points}, reduces peak contact force by \assumed{$X\%$}, and shortens completion time by \assumed{$X\%$} relative to the strongest baseline.
\end{abstract}
