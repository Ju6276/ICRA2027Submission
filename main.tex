\begin{abstract}
Contact-rich manipulation requires physical corrections on a shorter timescale than semantic task planning. Existing force-conditioned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, however, couple force feedback with complete action generation, preventing the physical correction from being updated independently of generative inference. We present ForceDelta-VLA, a slow--fast framework that extracts an explicit force-responsive correction from a pretrained force-aware VLA and distills it into a compact residual policy. For the same visual--language context and robot state, a frozen teacher predicts actions under the measured force history and a learned null-force reference mode. Their difference, evaluated under matched stochastic conditions, provides a model-level counterfactual residual target without task rewards or additional corrective demonstrations. At deployment, the VLA updates reference actions at the semantic planning rate, while the residual policy asynchronously incorporates the latest force measurements to correct the commanded pose. Across eight single-arm and bimanual contact-rich tasks, ForceDelta-VLA improves mean task success by $X$ percentage points, reduces peak contact force by $X\%$, and shortens task completion time by $X\%$ relative to the strongest baseline.
\end{abstract}
