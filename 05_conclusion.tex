We introduced ForceDelta-VLA, a force-aware VLA that replaces reactive full-action generation with counterfactual residual distillation. Paired factual-force and adapted null-force predictions of a frozen teacher define the residual target, while the null-mode prediction serves as the slow reference. A lightweight causal student then predicts pose corrections from native-rate force history, cached task context, robot state, and the current reference. This decomposition turns the reactive pathway from an iterative generative policy into a deterministic residual predictor.

The counterfactual in ForceDelta-VLA is defined at the model level rather than as a causal intervention. Because the null query uses a learned token and a null-specific adapter, and because vision and state may also reveal contact, the resulting residual is not a unique force contribution. The approach also inherits teacher error and the coverage limits of offline demonstrations, and discrete strategy changes may not be representable by an additive pose correction. Future work should combine counterfactual residual distillation with limited on-policy correction and analyze stability and safety under delayed asynchronous composition.
