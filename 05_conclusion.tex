We presented FoRe-VLA, a teacher-guided framework for distilling force-conditioned action residuals for contact-rich manipulation. The central hypothesis is that the behavioral responsibility of nominal and force-reactive branches should be supervised explicitly rather than induced only by architectural separation. By querying the same frozen temporal force-aware teacher with measured force history and a learned null-force token, FoRe-VLA obtains a non-force-conditioned reference and derives a complementary policy residual through teacher output differencing. These targets supervise a vision--language--state policy that produces nominal action chunks and semantic intent, and a lightweight policy that uses live high-frequency force feedback to update corrective residuals. The teacher and null token are required only to construct training targets; force sensing remains active during deployment.

Our experimental design evaluates whether this explicit decomposition preserves the teacher's task-level behavior while improving responsiveness and physical-interaction quality in contact-rich manipulation. In particular, the planned comparisons test the contributions of native-rate temporal force encoding, learned null-force conditioning, teacher-derived residual supervision, and asynchronous fast-policy execution. Beyond overall task success, the evaluation emphasizes contact-specific measures such as peak force, safety-limit violations, disturbance-recovery success, and correction latency.

Several limitations remain. Full-minus-null teacher differencing is a policy-space residual, not contrastive learning or a guarantee of causal force attribution. Its usefulness depends on the temporal teacher and on whether the learned null token provides a meaningful non-force-conditioned reference without creating an out-of-distribution query. Moreover, additive correction may be insufficient for interactions that require discrete strategy changes, and asynchronous composition requires careful treatment of delay, stability, and safety constraints. Future work will investigate causal and uncertainty-aware decompositions, adaptive scheduling, safety-constrained residual control, and evaluation across a broader range of robots, sensors, and contact-rich tasks.
