ForceDelta-VLA distills force and staleness residuals from cached-context teacher queries, rebases the known reference-state displacement deterministically, and executes the learned corrections independently of the generative reference. On nine real-robot tasks, it reaches an $82.2\%$ mean success rate and lowers peak contact force by about $16\%$ and $19\%$ relative to ImplicitRDP on the single-arm and bimanual platforms, respectively, while recording the shortest mean successful-trial completion time on both. Its residual forward pass fits within the model-inference budget associated with the platform's $100$-Hz target-update limit, while the generative reference is refreshed asynchronously. Ablations favor correctly paired force observations, cached-context teacher targets, decomposed residual prediction, and reference-anchored execution.

A pose residual cannot replace an invalid reference or perform nonlocal recovery, and the method assumes that cached visual context remains locally valid within a reference interval. Reliance on robot-estimated wrench may also limit sensitivity to weak contact. Future work includes confidence-triggered reference refresh, lightweight current-visual updates, richer contact sensing, and recovery policies trained with corrective rollouts.
