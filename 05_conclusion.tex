We introduced ForceRes-VLA, a slow--fast force-aware policy in which the reactive branch predicts a teacher-defined action residual instead of regenerating a complete action chunk. Paired full/null queries of one frozen teacher provide explicit conditional-residual supervision, while the null-mode prediction serves as the slow reference. A lightweight causal student then updates pose corrections from native-rate force history, cached task context, robot state, and reference age. The measured implementation reduces high-rate inference from $76.1$~ms for a ten-step cached-prefix action expert to $2.4$~ms for one residual update.

The method deliberately makes a limited claim about its decomposition. Because the null query uses a learned token and a null-specific adapter, and because vision and state may also reveal contact, the paired difference is a conditional deviation of the teacher rather than a causal or unique force contribution. Its usefulness must therefore be established empirically through contact localization, force ablation, composed-policy fidelity, and robot outcomes. The approach also inherits teacher error and the coverage limits of offline demonstrations; discrete strategy changes may not be representable by an additive pose correction. Future work should combine the residual target with limited on-policy correction and analyze stability and safety under delayed asynchronous composition.
