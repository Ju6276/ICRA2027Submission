ForceDelta-VLA constructs force and reference-state-consistent staleness residual targets from cached-context teacher predictions and executes the distilled corrections independently of the generative reference. Across nine real-robot tasks, the method achieves $82.2\%$ mean success, a $27.8$-percentage-point improvement over ForceVLA. It also reduces mean successful-trial peak contact force by approximately $26\%$ relative to ForceVLA on both platforms and records the lowest mean successful-trial completion time among the evaluated methods. The residual forward pass takes $2.43$~ms. Replacing teacher-difference supervision, combining the residual heads, or omitting schedule replay lowers success in the ablation evaluations.

Performance is limited when an inaccurate reference or a required recovery motion exceeds the bounded correction range. The fast pathway also relies on cached visual context remaining locally valid within a reference interval. Reliance on robot-estimated wrench may also limit sensitivity to weak contact. Future work includes confidence-triggered reference refresh, lightweight current-visual updates, richer contact sensing, and recovery policies trained with corrective rollouts.
