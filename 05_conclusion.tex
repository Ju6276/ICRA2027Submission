% \section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a framework for real-time multi-modal trajectory generation in robotic manipulation. A Conditional Flow Matching teacher is first used to model expert trajectory distributions conditioned on a unified perceptual embedding. To reduce the latency of ODE-based flow sampling, we distill the teacher into a single-step student using a sample-based, likelihood-free objective inspired by Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE). In practice, this objective is instantiated as a set-level bi-directional Chamfer matching loss between sampled teacher and student trajectory sets.

By maintaining architectural consistency between the multi-step teacher and the single-step student, our experiments empirically show that the proposed distillation objective can compress the generative process while largely preserving task performance. The student policy retains diverse trajectory modes of the expert distribution in practice and substantially mitigates the mode-collapse behaviors observed in naive single-step methods and standard distillation baselines. Experiments in both simulation and dynamic real-world scenarios show that the distilled policy achieves a $43\times$ speedup, operating at approximately 125 Hz, while maintaining competitive success rates and enabling reactive closed-loop manipulation under human-induced disturbances.

Several limitations remain. First, external baselines are evaluated using their original architectures and sensory configurations when possible, so these results should be interpreted as end-to-end comparisons rather than loss-only ablations under an identical multimodal backbone. Second, although our RLBench evaluation follows the eight-task setup of PointFlowMatch for direct comparability, it does not yet cover highly contact-rich or precision-demanding tasks such as peg insertion or cup tree. Third, our current qualitative analysis focuses mainly on execution outcomes and failure modes; more detailed teacher--student trajectory visualizations would further clarify how set-level distillation affects trajectory diversity. Future work will investigate broader distillation-loss comparisons, improved set-level objectives, hybrid single-/multi-step refinement, and evaluation on more contact-rich manipulation benchmarks.
