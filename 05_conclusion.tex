We presented FoRe-VLA, which decomposes a force-aware manipulation policy by \emph{attribution} rather than by architecture. Existing slow--fast force policies cache the vision--language prefix but retain an action generator in the high-rate loop, and consequently pay a full flow-matching denoise per update. We observe that this branch need only produce the component of the action attributable to force, and obtain a label for that component by querying a frozen temporal force-aware teacher twice on the same observation, with the measured force window and with a learned null-force condition, under a shared vision--language prefix and a shared flow-matching noise draw. The resulting target is simple enough that a single deterministic forward pass through a small network suffices, reducing the cost of the high-rate path by $31.8\times$ and admitting execution above $400$~Hz. Randomizing the slow period, latency, and jitter during target generation, and conditioning the student on its measured context age, allows one trained student to be deployed across rates unseen in training.

Two limitations bound the interpretation of these results. Full-minus-null differencing is a conditional difference in policy space rather than a causal force attribution, since vision and state condition both branches and may themselves carry contact cues; we therefore claim only that the deviation is the teacher's output change under full versus null force conditioning. The construction is also bounded above by the teacher, whose imitation error is inherited by any student distilled from it, which is why no behavior-cloning term is placed on the student.

Prior work on residual learning for contact-rich manipulation reports that contact feedback induces a policy-dependent distribution shift which demonstration data does not cover, and that on-policy corrective rollouts are valuable in this regime~\cite{xu2025crdagger,lv2026lift}. Our target is generated offline and inherits that limitation, with the compensating property that it requires neither robot interaction nor human corrections. Whether an offline counterfactual target suffices, or is best combined with a limited on-policy phase, is the most immediate direction for future work. Additive correction may further be insufficient for interactions requiring discrete strategy changes, and asynchronous composition warrants explicit treatment of delay, stability, and safety limits.
