We presented ForceDelta-VLA, a correction-distillation framework for contact-rich manipulation. Paired force-conditioned and force-agnostic teacher predictions define force-correction targets, while delay-correction targets account for reference-action mismatch and state rebasing. The correction policy is trained with asynchronous schedule replay and updates pose corrections between reference-action updates. Across nine real-robot tasks, ForceDelta-VLA achieved $82.2\%$ mean success, compared with $54.4\%$ for ForceVLA and $70.6\%$ for the Temporal Teacher. Mean successful-trial peak contact force was approximately $26\%$ lower than ForceVLA on both platforms.

The method relies on locally valid task context and reference actions that can be adjusted within the correction bounds. Interactions requiring a different approach or recovery motion may exceed this range. Future work will investigate reference-action refresh and recovery strategies for these cases.
