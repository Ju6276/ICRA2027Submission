We presented ForceDelta-VLA, a residual-distillation framework that constructs force and staleness corrections from cached-context teacher queries and executes them independently of the generative reference. Across nine real-robot tasks, it achieves an $82.2\%$ mean success rate and reduces peak contact force by approximately $16\%$ and $19\%$ relative to ImplicitRDP on the single-arm and bimanual platforms, respectively. It also records the lowest mean successful-trial completion time on both platforms. Its $2.43$-ms residual inference occupies about a quarter of a $100$-Hz control cycle, whereas the reference path requires $189.7$~ms, so local correction fits inside the command loop while the cached reference is refreshed asynchronously. The force-input diagnostics show sensitivity to temporally aligned wrench history, while the dual-head and schedule-replay ablations provide directional support for the decomposition.

The method remains bounded by the locality and coverage of its cached reference: a pose residual cannot replace an invalid task-level plan or perform nonlocal recovery such as retreat, re-approach, or strategy switching. The fast pathway also assumes that cached visual context remains valid within a reference interval, limiting its response to scene changes not reflected in robot state or wrench. Finally, robot-estimated wrench restricts sensitivity to subtle contact events. Future work will combine confidence-triggered reference refresh, lightweight current-visual updates, richer contact sensing, and recovery policies trained with corrective rollouts.
