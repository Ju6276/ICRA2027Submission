We presented ForceDelta-VLA, a policy-decomposition framework that identifies a teacher-defined force-responsive correction, compensates for stale references, and executes the correction independently of full-action generation. Matched full-force and learned null-force predictions define the correction target without task rewards or additional corrective or on-policy demonstrations. Staleness-aware distillation compensates for delayed references, and the independently executable residual pathway updates pose corrections from recent force history without regenerating complete actions. \assumed{Final text will summarize how the real-robot results validate the decomposition through task performance, contact quality, residual diagnostics, and delayed-reference robustness.}

The method remains limited by the accuracy and coverage of its offline teacher, while its bounded additive residual cannot represent discrete strategy changes. Moreover, the paired target is a model-defined conditional difference rather than a causal attribution of force, and asynchronous composition has no formal safety guarantee under variable delay. Future work will incorporate limited on-policy correction, discrete recovery decisions, and delay-aware safety constraints.
